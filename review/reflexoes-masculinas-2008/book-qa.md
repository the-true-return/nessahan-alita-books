## Open questions

- **00-0001**: Series title follows the glossary proposal "Man’s Suffering in Love" (O Sofrimento Amoroso do Homem), as in How to Deal with Women.
- **00-0003**: Subtitle "Algumas Heresias que Faltaram Dizer" (glossary proposal). Alternatives: "Some Heresies That Remained to Be Said", "Some Heresies That Went Unsaid".
- **00-0006**: The citation gives the English titles; the Portuguese originals are "O Sofrimento Amoroso do Homem" and "Reflexões Masculinas sobre a Mulher e o Amor (Algumas Heresias que Faltaram Dizer)".
- **00-0012**: The author’s notice forbids any alteration of the book’s "language or title" (unlike Volume I, it does not name translation explicitly). Translated faithfully; a human should confirm that this English edition is authorized before publication.
- **00-0029**: The table of contents numbers this chapter 6, but in the body it is numbered "5." (a second chapter 5, p. 46), and the chapter "6. Algumas artimanhas femininas infernizantes" (p. 52) is missing from this list. Kept as in the original (see known_issues).
- **01-0003**: The original spells "Karen Salmanshon"; corrected to Salmansohn, as in How to Deal with Women. Amy Sutherland’s book is presumably "What Shamu Taught Me About Life, Love, and Marriage" (2008), which grew out of her 2006 New York Times article; the author does not name it.
- **02-0007**: "misóginos e androfóbicos-misândricos espertinhos": the author’s compound "androfóbico-misândrico" rendered "androphobic misandrists"; "espertinhos" (masculine of the glossary’s "espertinha(s)" → "sly one(s)") rendered "sly".
- **02-0018**: The original list jumps from 15 to 17 (there is no item 16). Numbering kept as in the original.
- **03-0001**: "aprisionamento à mulher" = a man’s being imprisoned (bound) to a woman. Rendered "imprisonment to woman" to keep the author’s prison imagery ("A ligação é uma prisão"). Alternatives: "The ways a man is held captive by a woman", "The types of bondage to woman".
- **04-0013**: Lévi, Dogma and Ritual of High Magic (1855), translated from the author’s Portuguese, which uses the archaic "tu" (hence "thee/thy", as in How to Deal with Women) and "vós" in the woman’s words (rendered "you"). Bracketed insertions are the author’s. "LËVI" in the original corrected to "LÉVI". Compare Waite’s public-domain "Transcendental Magic" (1896).
- **04-0016**: "mulheres que amam demais" alludes to Robin Norwood’s "Women Who Love Too Much" (1985), whose established English title is used.
- **04-0021**: "phalus" kept as in the original (Latin-style spelling, italic); the standard spelling is "phallus".
- **04-0025**: "matrixianos" (the author’s coinage from the film The Matrix: men who live inside the conventional system without awareness) rendered "Matrixian". Alternatives: "Matrix-bound", "plugged-in".
- **05-0016**: "impressionismo" is the author’s word for the art of making an impression; kept as "impressionism" (also in 05-0018). Alternative: "making an impression".
- **05-0018**: "à desatenção exclusiva por parte de um homem" read as a man’s inattention singling her out ("singular inattention"). Alternative: "inattention from one particular man".
- **05-0021**: Portuguese "atingir" means both "to reach/affect" and "to hit"; the author’s disclaimer plays on the second sense. Rendered "reach" (heading and here), which loses the "hit" ambiguity. Alternative: "strike", which keeps the violent sense the author disclaims.
- **05-0030**: Film cited by its Brazilian title "Hitch: Conselheiro Amoroso"; the original title is "Hitch" (Andy Tennant, 2005).
- **05-0032**: Idiom "não dão agulhadas sem dedal" (lit. "they don’t ply the needle without a thimble") rendered "they never take a risk without protection".
- **05-0039**: "algum vadio" (masculine of the glossary’s "vadia" → "tramp") rendered "tramp", consistent with 02-0016 ("homens vadios" → "male tramps").
- **05-0053**: Nietzsche’s Übermensch ("Além do Homem" in the author’s Portuguese) rendered "Overman". Alternatives: "Superman", "Beyond-Man".
- **06-0004**: "dissolução do eu" rendered "dissolution of the I" (Gurdjieff/Samael Aun Weor usage; cf. glossary "eus" → "I’s"). Alternative: "dissolution of the self". "battered man syndrome" is in English in the original.
- **06-0006**: Idiom "esmurrando facas" (from "dar murro em ponta de faca", lit. "punching the point of a knife": a futile, self-harming struggle) rendered "banging our heads against a wall".
- **07-0001**: Numbered "5." in the original, although the previous chapter is also "5." and the table of contents lists this one as 6. Kept as in the original (see known_issues).
- **07-0015**: "inferno astral" (Brazilian astrological expression for a period of bad luck; here simply "torment") rendered "sheer hell".
- **08-0001**: This chapter does not appear in the original’s table of contents, and its number "6." coincides with the number the contents give to the previous chapter. Kept as in the original (see known_issues). "infernizantes" (that make one’s life hell) rendered "hellish"; alternative: "tormenting".
- **08-0022**: "Salmanshon" in the original corrected to Salmansohn (as in 01-0003).
- **08-0028**: "fêmea fatal" rendered "fatal female" (approved glossary term "female"), as in How to Deal with Women; alternative: "femme fatale".
- **09-0002**: "s/d" (sem data) rendered "n.d." (no date). The work is Alberoni’s "L’erotismo" (1986), listed in the bibliography as "Eroticism" (Brazilian edition, n.d.).
- **11-0002**: Title translated from the Brazilian edition ("Dogma e Ritual de Alta Magia"), as in the other books. Waite’s English translation is "Transcendental Magic: Its Doctrine and Ritual" (1896).
- **11-0003**: Brazilian edition "O Erotismo: Fantasias e Realidades do Amor e da Sedução", as in How to Deal with Women; the Italian original is "L’erotismo" (1986). "sem data" rendered "n.d.".
- **11-0005**: Brazilian release title "Hitch: Conselheiro Amoroso"; original title "Hitch".
