## Open questions

- **00-0001**: Title "Textos Complementares" → "Supplementary Texts" (glossary, config title_en). Alternative: "Complementary Texts".
- **00-0004**: The citation gives the English title; the Portuguese original is "Textos Complementares". "Edição virtual independente" → "Independent online edition", as in the other volumes.
- **02-0001**: "Aprofundamentos" (literally "deepenings"): a set of short notes that deepen points from the earlier books. Used "Going deeper"; alternatives: "Deeper insights", "Further explorations".
- **02-0017**: "virar o barco" (literally "turn the boat around"): to reverse the situation. Kept the image; alternative: "turn the tables".
- **02-0022**: "Estreitamento da intimidade" (literally "narrowing of intimacy"): growing closer, increasing intimacy. Heading "Narrowing the distance of intimacy"; alternatives: "Deepening intimacy", "Growing closer".
- **02-0081**: "O Alienista" → "The Alienist" (established English title of Machado de Assis’s novella). "O Imbecil Coletivo" (Olavo de Carvalho, 1996) has no established English edition; translated as "The Collective Imbecile".
- **02-0102**: Wordplay: "desgraçados" means "wretches," and the author reads it as "des-graçados," "deprived of Grace." Rendered "dis-graced wretches" to keep the pun; alternative: "We human beings are wretches (deprived of the Grace of the Spirit)" with a TN.
- **02-0113**: "sangue de barata" (literally "cockroach blood"): Brazilian idiom for imperturbable coolness. Rendered "ice water in our veins"; alternative: "a cool head".
- **04-0006**: "não queria dar o braço a torcer" (idiom: refuse to concede or admit being wrong) rendered "did not want to admit it"; alternative: "did not want to give in".
- **06-0008**: The original spells the English term "batttered man syndrome" (typo); corrected to "battered man syndrome," as the author writes it in 06-0019.
- **06-0009**: The original note reads "Em português: 'síndrome do homem maltratado'" ("In Portuguese: 'syndrome of the mistreated man'"), glossing the English term for Brazilian readers. Rendered as a gloss in English so no Portuguese remains; alternative: drop the "In Portuguese" framing as done, or have a TN explain that the term was in English in the original.
- **08-0004**: The original spells the researcher’s name "Johnasson" (also in chapter 22); corrected to Peter K. Jonason. "Tríade sinistra" (literally "sinister triad") rendered with the established English term "Dark Triad" (narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy); alternative: "sinister triad".
- **08-0006**: "matrimônio perfeito" alludes to Samael Aun Weor’s "The Perfect Matrimony" (O Matrimônio Perfeito), the title used in The Profane Feminine; rendered "the perfect matrimony."
- **10-0007**: The original spells the Latin "phalus erectus"; corrected to "phallus erectus."
- **16-0006**: "dar trela" (colloquial: to encourage, give rope to someone’s advances) rendered "give the time of day"; alternatives: "lead on", "encourage".
- **17-0006**: "esmurrando a faca" (literally "punching the knife"; Brazilian idiom for a futile, self-harming struggle) kept literally since the sentence explains it; alternative: "kicking against the pricks".
- **20-0003**: "molecas" (girls acting like brats/tomboys, i.e. childish) rendered "brats"; alternatives "silly little girls", "kids".
- **21-0004**: "esmurra facas" plays on the Brazilian idiom "dar murro em ponta de faca" (to struggle uselessly against something unbeatable, cf. "kick against the pricks"); kept the image literally. Alternative: "kicks against the pricks".
- **22-0005**: Original spells "Eric From"; corrected to "Erich Fromm".
- **22-0013**: "V.M." (Venerável Mestre, "Venerable Master") kept as the abbreviation used in Gnostic literature. Book title "Morte em Marcha" translated literally as "Death in Motion"; no established English title confirmed (alternatives: "Death on the March", "Death Underway"). The original ends with a doubled period "..", treated as a typo.
- **23-0010**: Original spells "Johnasson" (heading 23-0009 and here, "Peter Johnasson"); corrected to "Jonason" (Peter K. Jonason), as in 08-0004. The original cites the book by its short title "Reflexões Masculinas"; the full English title is used per project rules.
