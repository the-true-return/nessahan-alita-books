## Open questions

- **00-0001**: Series title follows the glossary proposal "Man’s Suffering in Love" (O Sofrimento Amoroso do Homem), as in the other volumes.
- **00-0006**: The citation gives the English titles; the Portuguese originals are "O Sofrimento Amoroso do Homem: O Profano Feminino (Considerações sobre a Face da Mulher que Ninguém Quer Encarar)".
- **00-0012**: The author’s notice forbids any alteration of the book’s "language or title" (this 2005 edition does not list "translate" explicitly, unlike the 2008 edition). Translated faithfully; a human must confirm that this English edition is authorized before publication (as for the other volumes).
- **00-0033**: The table of contents reads "Como elas minam a desconfiança" (How they undermine distrust), but the chapter heading itself is "O enfraquecimento das desconfianças" (The weakening of suspicions). Both kept as in the original, as in the 2008 edition.
- **01-0007**: "convivência e à conquista" → "living together and winning a woman over" (as in the 2008 edition; wording tightened so the Introduction fits on one page). Alternative: "coexistence and courtship".
- **02-0006**: "dar-lhe um 'escracho'" (Brazilian slang: a humiliating put-down or public scolding) → “dressing-down”, as in the 2008 edition. Alternatives: “put-down”, “telling-off”. "dar uma de perseguida" → "play the pursued one".
- **02-0015**: "um 'Além do homem'" (Nietzsche’s Übermensch in Portuguese) → “Beyond-Man,” keeping the literal image, as in the 2008 edition. In Masculine Reflections on Woman and Love the same term was rendered “Overman”; alternatives: “Overman”, “Superman”.
- **02-0027**: "'o cara', 'o tal', 'o bom'" (colloquial praise) → “the man,” “the one,” “the real deal,” as in the 2008 edition. Alternatives: “the guy,” “the big shot,” “the good one.”
- **02-0029**: The original spells "Karen Salmanshon"; corrected to the usual spelling, Karen Salmansohn (American self-help author). Amy Sutherland is the author of "What Shamu Taught Me About Life, Love, and Marriage".
- **02-0030**: "Certo(a)s idiotas", and below "outro(a)s imbecis" and "esse(a)s idiotas": the author marks both genders with "(a)"; rendered with "male and female" here and kept implicit in the later instances (as in the 2008 edition).
- **02-0031**: "esses três atrativos" ("these three attractions") follows four listed items (certainties, definitions, clarity, transparency); it may refer to sex, affection, and love (named in 02-0032). Kept as in the original.
- **02-0036**: "asnos (e mulas)" → "jackasses (and she-mules)", keeping the masculine/feminine pairing. "machismo" is kept, since English uses the word; the author’s etymological point ("macho" = male) is glossed in parentheses as "macho (male)".
- **03-0006**: "fêmeas fatais" → "fatal females", keeping the approved "female" (fêmea) and the author’s play on "femme fatale". Alternative: "femmes fatales".
- **03-0008**: "as gostosonas" (slang augmentative: sexy, curvy women) → "the hotties". Alternative: "the sexiest babes".
- **03-0009**: The original says "as 24% restantes" after 74% (the remainder would be 26%). Kept "24%" as in the original; possibly a slip of the author.
- **03-0024**: "phalus erectus" is already Latin in the original and kept as written (the usual spelling would be "phallus"). "quatro metros" kept in meters (about 13 feet).
- **04-0003**: "Van Creveld" is presumably Martin van Creveld, the Israeli military historian; the argument matches his "Men, Women and War" (2001), later expanded in "The Privileged Sex" (2013). Kept as in the original.
- **05-0002**: "trocam os 'R' pelos 'L'" describes Portuguese baby talk; kept literally ("swap their R’s for L’s"). An English equivalent would be swapping R’s for W’s ("wabbit").
- **05-0009**: "o promíscuo 'ficar'" (Brazilian slang for casual making out or sex without commitment) → “hooking up.” "segurar o rojão" (to bear the brunt) → "hold the bag".
- **06-0007**: "Aqueles que as 'dobram'" (those who "fold"/"bend" them, i.e., who win them over and make them yield) → “bend.” Alternative: “win them over.”
- **06-0013**: "'o gostosão' ou 'o bonzão'" (slang augmentatives: the sexy guy, the great guy) → “the hunk” or “the top dog.” "Kids" is Larry Clark’s 1995 film.
- **06-0021**: "um 'banana'" (Brazilian slang for a spineless, weak man) → “wimp.” Alternative: “pushover.”
- **08-0013**: Presumably Goleman’s discussion of male "stonewalling" in "Emotional Intelligence" (1995), after John Gottman; no source is given in the original.
- **09-0008**: "a irritação da dúvida, como diz Peirce" refers to Charles S. Peirce’s "irritation of doubt" ("The Fixation of Belief", 1877), the established English phrase.
- **10-0011**: The Schopenhauer line is translated from the author’s Portuguese; the source passage in Schopenhauer was not identified, so no published English wording was used.
- **15-0079**: The source spells “Warren Farrel”; corrected to the author’s actual name, Warren Farrell.

## Proposed translator's notes

- **10-0034**: “Inferno astral” is a common Brazilian expression (from popular astrology) for a stretch of bad luck and troubles; rendered literally as “astral hell,” since the author also uses it in its esoteric sense of an inner, psychic hell.
