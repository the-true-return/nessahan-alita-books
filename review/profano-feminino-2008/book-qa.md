## Open questions

- **00-0001**: Series title follows the glossary proposal "Man’s Suffering in Love" (O Sofrimento Amoroso do Homem), as in the other volumes.
- **00-0002**: Title "O Profano Feminino" → "The Profane Feminine" (glossary, config title_en); it echoes, by contrast, the "Sacred Feminine."
- **00-0003**: Subtitle "Considerações sobre uma Face da Mulher que Ninguém quer Encarar". "Considerações" → "Reflections"; alternatives: "Considerations on a Face of Woman No One Wants to Face", "Thoughts on a Side of Women Nobody Wants to Confront".
- **00-0006**: The citation gives the English titles; the Portuguese originals are "O Profano Feminino: Considerações sobre uma Face da Mulher que Ninguém Quer Encarar" and "O Sofrimento Amoroso do Homem - Vol. II".
- **00-0014**: The author’s notice explicitly forbids translating the book and any alteration of its "language or title". Translated faithfully; a human must confirm that this English edition is authorized before publication (as for the other volumes).
- **00-0036**: The table of contents reads "Como elas minam a desconfiança" (How they undermine distrust), but the chapter heading itself is "O enfraquecimento das desconfianças" (The weakening of suspicions). Both kept as in the original.
- **01-0007**: "convivência e à conquista" → "living together and with winning a woman over". Alternative: "coexistence and courtship".
- **02-0006**: "dar-lhe um 'escracho'" (Brazilian slang: a humiliating put-down or public scolding) → “dressing-down”. Alternatives: “put-down”, “telling-off”. "dar uma de perseguida" → "play the pursued one".
- **02-0015**: "um 'Além do homem'" (Nietzsche’s Übermensch in Portuguese) → “Beyond-Man,” keeping the literal image. In Masculine Reflections on Woman and Love the same term was rendered “Overman”; alternatives: “Overman”, “Superman”.
- **02-0027**: "'o cara', 'o tal', 'o bom'" (colloquial praise) → “the man,” “the one,” “the real deal.” Alternatives: “the guy,” “the big shot,” “the good one.”
- **02-0029**: The original spells "Karen Salmanshon"; corrected to the usual spelling, Karen Salmansohn (American self-help author). Amy Sutherland is the author of "What Shamu Taught Me About Life, Love, and Marriage".
- **02-0030**: "Certo(a)s idiotas", and below "outro(a)s imbecis" and "esse(a)s idiotas": the author marks both genders with "(a)"; rendered with "male and female" here and kept implicit in the later instances.
- **02-0031**: "esses três atrativos" ("these three attractions") follows four listed items (certainties, definitions, clarity, transparency); it may refer to sex, affection, and love (named in 02-0032). Kept as in the original.
- **02-0036**: "asnos (e mulas)" → "jackasses (and she-mules)", keeping the masculine/feminine pairing. "machismo" is kept, since English uses the word; the author’s etymological point ("macho" = male) is glossed in parentheses as "macho (male)".
- **03-0006**: "fêmeas fatais" → "fatal females", keeping the approved "female" (fêmea) and the author’s play on "femme fatale". Alternative: "femmes fatales".
- **03-0008**: "as gostosonas" (slang augmentative: sexy, curvy women) → "the hotties". Alternative: "the sexiest babes".
- **03-0009**: The original says "as 24% restantes" after 74% (the remainder would be 26%). Kept "24%" as in the original; possibly a slip of the author.
- **03-0024**: "phalus erectus" is already Latin in the original and kept as written (the usual spelling would be "phallus"). "quatro metros" kept in meters (about 13 feet).
- **04-0003**: "Van Creveld" is presumably Martin van Creveld, the Israeli military historian; the argument matches his "Men, Women and War" (2001), later expanded in "The Privileged Sex" (2013). Not listed in the bibliography; kept as in the original.
- **05-0002**: "trocam os 'R' pelos 'L'" describes Portuguese baby talk; kept literally ("swap their R’s for L’s"). An English equivalent would be swapping R’s for W’s ("wabbit").
- **05-0009**: "o promíscuo 'ficar'" (Brazilian slang for casual making out or sex without commitment) → “hooking up.” "segurar o rojão" (to bear the brunt) → "hold the bag".
- **06-0007**: "Aqueles que as 'dobram'" (those who "fold"/"bend" them, i.e., who win them over and make them yield) → “bend.” Alternative: “win them over.”
- **06-0013**: "'o gostosão' ou 'o bonzão'" (slang augmentatives: the sexy guy, the great guy) → “the hunk” or “the top dog.” "Kids" is Larry Clark’s 1995 film.
- **06-0021**: "um 'banana'" (Brazilian slang for a spineless, weak man) → “wimp.” Alternative: “pushover.”
- **08-0013**: Presumably Goleman’s discussion of male "stonewalling" in "Emotional Intelligence" (1995), after John Gottman; no source is given in the original.
- **09-0008**: "a irritação da dúvida, como diz Peirce" refers to Charles S. Peirce’s "irritation of doubt" ("The Fixation of Belief", 1877), the established English phrase.
- **09-0031**: The original reads "Slayne, o herói celta de Path Mills"; corrected to Sláine, the Celtic hero of Pat Mills’s comic series (2000 AD). The quoted lines are translated from the author’s Portuguese; the original English wording of the comic was not checked.
- **10-0011**: The Schopenhauer line is translated from the author’s Portuguese; the source passage in Schopenhauer was not identified, so no published English wording was used.
- **15-0002**: Book titles translated from the author’s Portuguese. “Tratado de Psicologia Revolucionária” and “O Matrimônio Perfeito” are known in English as “Treatise of Revolutionary Psychology” and “The Perfect Matrimony” (as in How to Deal with Women). “Dialética da Razão Objetiva” rendered literally as “Dialectic of Objective Reason”; no established English title was identified (possibly the work published as “The Dialectic of Consciousness” / “Introduction to Gnosis”; to be checked).
- **15-0003**: Title translated from the Brazilian edition (“Dogma e Ritual de Alta Magia”), as in the other books; Waite’s English translation is “Transcendental Magic: Its Doctrine and Ritual” (1896).
- **15-0009**: Fromm’s categories are given as the author lists them (from the Brazilian translation); they do not correspond exactly to the headings of the English original of “The Art of Loving” (1956).
- **17-0007**: “(rs)” is the Brazilian internet abbreviation for “risos” (laughter); rendered “(ha ha)” here and in 17-0012. Alternative: “(lol)” or omitting it. The Lévi phrase is translated from the author’s Portuguese; compare Waite’s public-domain “Transcendental Magic” (1896).
- **18-0069**: Lalande’s “Vocabulaire technique et critique de la philosophie” (French original), quoted in the author’s own Portuguese translation; translated here from his Portuguese. “(p. 763, tradução minha)” kept as the author’s statement (“my translation”). “sub. Vº” (sub verbo) rendered as “s.v.” The same applies to 18-0070 to 18-0073 and 18-0077.
- **18-0079**: “NOVA ENCICLOPÉDIA BARSA” rendered “NEW BARSA ENCYCLOPEDIA” and, in 18-0080, “GRANDE ENCICLOPÉDIA LAROUSSE CULTURAL” rendered “GREAT LAROUSSE CULTURAL ENCYCLOPEDIA” (Brazilian encyclopedias; the quotations are translated from the Portuguese). Keep consistent with the bibliography (chapter 20).
- **18-0083**: Encyclopedia quotation translated from the Portuguese; the bracketed insertions are the author’s.
- **18-0104**: The original prints the French title as "Du Fundament de l’Induction"; Lachelier's work is "Du fondement de l’induction" (1871). Corrected the spelling and kept the title in French, as in the original.
- **18-0115**: The original spells the title "Tratactus Theologico-Politicus"; corrected to Spinoza's "Tractatus Theologico-Politicus" (Latin, kept as in the original).
- **19-0005**: "certas ações justas especulares devolutivas refratárias ou desmascarantes" is an unpunctuated string of adjectives; read as just actions that mirror (cf. "movimento especular ou de retorno", 18-0065), give back, are refractory, or unmask. Alternative: "certain just mirror actions that give back, refract, or unmask."
- **20-0002**: Brazilian edition "O Erotismo: Fantasias e Realidades do Amor e da Sedução", rendered as in How to Deal with Women; the Italian original is "L’erotismo" (1986). "sem data" rendered "n.d.".
- **20-0004**: "GRANDE ENCICLOPÉIDA [sic] LAROUSSE CULTURAL" (typo in the original) rendered "GREAT LAROUSSE CULTURAL ENCYCLOPEDIA", as in the body text (18-0080). Publisher names kept.
- **20-0006**: The author cites the Spanish edition; its title is kept in Spanish (the original prints "Vocabulário", corrected to Spanish "Vocabulario"). The French original is "Vocabulaire technique et critique de la philosophie". The Spanish phrase "Obra laureada por la Academia Francesa" and the Portuguese "Original da Sociedade Francesa de Filosofía" were translated.
- **20-0007**: "Encyclopaedia Britannica do Brasil Publicações Ltda." is the publisher's corporate name and is kept as a proper name.

## Proposed translator's notes

- **10-0034**: “Inferno astral” is a common Brazilian expression (from popular astrology) for a stretch of bad luck and troubles; rendered literally as “astral hell,” since the author also uses it in its esoteric sense of an inner, psychic hell.
