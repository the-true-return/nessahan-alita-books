15. The Wretched Feeling of Passion

<!-- seg 16-0002 paragraph h=d37ff0a9 -->
Let us begin this chapter with the Schopenhauerian definition of love:

<!-- seg 16-0003 paragraph h=7e2533bc -->
“Love is evil” (SCHOPENHAUER, 2004, p. 33).

<!-- seg 16-0004 paragraph h=b386a4f3 -->
Obviously, Schopenhauer is referring to romantic love, directed exclusively at one single woman, and not to universal love. When a love crisis is exceedingly intense, it can unleash an existential and spiritual crisis so deep that it leads the individual to re-evaluate his whole life and emerge renewed from this dark and dangerous passage (KORNFIELD, 1997 and GROF & GROF, 1989/1997). Unfortunately, the danger of getting lost on this journey forever also exists and is real. Crimes of passion are proof of this danger. Passionate love is hell after heaven.

<!-- seg 16-0005 paragraph h=15b17ecf -->
What we define here as romantic or passionate love (romantic passion) is what Erich Fromm (1976) calls “neurotic love” and also “pseudo-love,” one of the ills that afflict modern Western civilization, avid for possession and consumption. Still according to Fromm, neurotic love takes several forms: sentimental love, sadistic love, idolatrous love, and narcissistic love.

<!-- seg 16-0006 paragraph h=ee5024ba -->
I agree with Fromm. Western civilization is gravely ill, and one of its diseases is romantic love (which differs completely from true and conscious love), which is obsessive and possessive. The masculine form of expression of this neurotic love is the obsessive attempts to control, watch over, and forbid the other, while its feminine form of expression corresponds to the obsessive desire to induce the other person to fall deeply in love so as to have him at her feet. In both cases, there is the intention of subjugating the other person so that he or she does what we want. This is the war of passion, and it is within this sphere that freedom from passion is suggested here. Since the whole of modern Western civilization is sick, not a few people are affected by amorous insanities.

<!-- seg 16-0007 paragraph h=9840b3d4 -->
Review your love life history and you will probably discover that the ladies you loved most (in the sense we are dealing with here, that is, passionate love) did not love you, and those who loved you most were not equally loved by you. We then infer that it is fundamental not to fall passionately in love in order to make use of the woman’s passion for the benefit of the relationship. The first and fundamental capacity to be acquired is this: not falling passionately in love. Remember this above everything written in this book. Without this prerequisite, all the paths thought out here are useless and even dangerous. Do not try to put them into practice if you are in love, because the effects will fall back on you.

<!-- seg 16-0008 paragraph h=dc517609 -->
When we are in love, we spend immense amounts of energy trying to solve emotional puzzles, get out of labyrinths, and avoid traps. Terrible situations are created for us, and we suffer trying to get out of them in the best possible way. The result is weakening.

<!-- seg 16-0009 paragraph h=1fa1768e -->
Passion is like alcohol. It numbs consciousness, eliminates lucidity, prevents critical judgment, and causes hallucinations, making the beloved be seen as divine:[^1]

<!-- seg 16-0010 footnote n=1 h=0bdfa59b -->
The infatuated person is seized by a cognitive incapacity that does not allow him to see the real person with whom he has fallen in love. In her place, he sees the projection of an idealized archetypal image and firmly believes that the object of his love corresponds to his fantasy. “Being in love always brings comic phenomena to the person amid the tragic ones as well; and both because the person in love, possessed by the spirit of the species [instinct], comes to be dominated by this spirit and no longer belongs to himself.” (SCHOPENHAUER, 2004, pp. 35-36)

“Sexual love is always an illusion, since it is the result of an imaginary mirage” (LÉVI, 1855/2001, p. 111).

<!-- seg 16-0012 paragraph h=dc707976 -->
To fall passionately in love is to fall into disgrace, to lose one’s soul (ZUBATY, 2001), as happened to young Werther (GOETHE, 1774/1988). When the beloved loses the characteristics that make her attractive, she becomes uninteresting. Therefore, love, as we are dealing with it here, is malignant, hypocritical, self-interested, and selfish, because it is not directed at the other’s Being or Essence but rather at his or her physical, economic, or behavioral attractions. In practice, we find that women (and men too) are in no way up to true love, despite their absurd dreams of rose-colored romances, and they do not deserve it. When they dream deliriously of romances, they are actually dreaming of themselves, because there is nothing they see beyond their own feelings. Notice that the imbecile leading men of the ridiculous feminine romances in films and books give everything of themselves and receive very little in return, at most a few bland missionary-position sexual encounters, besides a few useless kisses. This is the absurd romantic dream that infects boys and makes them feeble as adults, making them believe that they will receive love, affection, and top-quality sex if they are nice, upright, faithful, hardworking, honest, and sincere.

<!-- seg 16-0013 paragraph h=559652c8 -->
Why does she come out unscathed after fighting with you? Why is she not disturbed? Simply because she skillfully reads in your behavior, through signs, that you are hooked, emotionally dependent. These are signs that communicate emotional dependence: jealousy, anger, sadness, curiosity about her conduct, fear of loss, being bothered by short skirts, daring necklines, etc. Instead of being bothered, simply show that you place no value on those who expose themselves to masculine desires while committed to you.

<!-- seg 16-0014 paragraph h=874fe8dc -->
To chain the male, the sly human female gives him affection, love, and good-quality sex until she feels he is well hooked and verifies his degree of dependence with many tests. When the poor wretch is well imprisoned and dependent, then he begins to be tortured to give her the pleasure of seeing him lost and disoriented, trying to find a way out. It is a sadistic test to measure our masculine worth. They know that we greatly need the affection and fragility that they possess.

<!-- seg 16-0015 paragraph h=bd502ae8 -->
Therefore, passion or romantic love is the nerve center of the male’s psychic enslavement. The main and most powerful weapon your partner has against you is your own feelings. Eliminate them to leave her powerless, or you will be thrown into an oscillating, alternating movement, exactly like the mouse between the cat’s claws, like a ping-pong ball. The ladies skillfully kindle contradictory feelings in us without the slightest fear of losing us: they provoke jealousy, then flatter us, etc.

<!-- seg 16-0016 paragraph h=52bec623 -->
The feeling of attachment in its various facets is an effective feminine tool for subjugating the male. The various faces of attachment are infatuation, jealousy, possessiveness, longing, fondness, and the fear of losing.

<!-- seg 16-0017 paragraph h=c13830a8 -->
To resist the feminine spell is above all to resist amorous feelings. Passion is the greatest danger and corresponds to a wretched state of servitude. In the Bible, this danger is clearly represented by the tragic fates of Adam (Genesis 3:1-24), Samson (Judges 16:1-22), David (II Samuel 11:1-27, 15:1-37, 18:9-33, and 19:1-10), and Solomon (I Kings 11:1-43).

<!-- seg 16-0018 paragraph h=580d2de3 -->
To train our resistance against passion, the best partner is the cunning manipulator, the emotional swindler who has no scruples about toying with and destroying other people’s feelings. If you are able to resist falling in love while exposing yourself to her fatal magnetism and defeat her, you will defeat any other.

The situation of the man in love is tragicomic:

<!-- seg 16-0020 paragraph h=1f7709c2 -->
When it is not yet established, passionate servitude is easier to avoid. However, once it is established, it can be removed only with great difficulty and suffering. As Nietzsche says (cited by SOUZA, 2003), the weak one and the slave are negated and destroyed within the wise man when the selective sieve of circular time acts. Of course this hurts a lot, but the prize makes up for the effort. The man becomes strong and superior:

<!-- seg 16-0021 paragraph h=da3530b6 -->

We would say, simply, that this is not very far from what we call a ‘seasoned’ person.” (Souza, 2003, p. 44)

To resist the enchantment of passion, one must hold back the imagination (LÉVI, 1855/2001) and the mind, not believe the sly one’s words, and not let oneself be fascinated by the charms of her delicate features and the fragility of her body. It is indispensable to resist the enchantment of tears and the sweetness of the voice. Skepticism, in this case, is an indispensable defense, and credulity a terrible weakness. Preserve your skepticism and deepen it. Never give wings to the first expectations and images that assail you when you see a beautiful woman.

<!-- seg 16-0024 paragraph h=dda0b4ef -->
All the feminine work I am describing consists in hooking the male through his feelings. Once he is hooked, they take him wherever they want, subjugate him, and, curiously, despise him deep down, considering him a weakling. They give themselves only to the strong men who feel nothing and resist all attempts at enchantment. It is for this reason that they never show explosions of passion for their own husbands when these are good, but only for the worst lovers. The good man is seen, from this feminine perspective, as an easily domesticated beast of burden. They decide in favor of the absurd because they are illogical (paradoxical) beings, or rather, beings who follow a logic of their own.

<!-- seg 16-0025 paragraph h=e0759f3c -->
The feminine attempt to enchant the male is in truth a test: the one who does not surrender shows himself to be the best.

<!-- seg 16-0026 paragraph h=1cffaa00 -->
In men, the pain of passion has its origin in childhood and bears many similarities to the childhood feelings caused by the absence of the mother. It is a feeling of helplessness, of never again finding another woman like her, which is absolutely irrational, since in today’s world there are approximately 3,000,000,000 women. The basic underlying idea with which the sly woman works in the masculine mind is that no other woman can replace her. This belief is continually reinforced without our noticing it, to our emotional misfortune.

<!-- seg 16-0027 paragraph h=d204d85d -->
The physical and psychic constitution of the woman we are dealing with here is adapted and prepared to extract physical, vital, and psychic forces from the man. They are natural vampires endowed with sophisticated energy-sucking powers.[^2] On the other hand, the feminine figure is necessary to our virility because it excites the masculine organs and activates their energy production. It follows, therefore, that women in themselves are not exclusively good or bad for man, but can be both at the same time. From this contradictory nature, which weakens and strengthens at the same time, arises the need to dominate them[^3] (in a magnetic sense, obviously, and never in an absurd sense of macho brutality) by means of their own fantasies, allowing her to live out her absurd dreams without, however, identifying ourselves with the roles we take on in these dreams. If we do not exercise dominance, in the sense already explained, they will be the ones who dominate us. Then they will look for other, stronger and more dominant males, because what interests her is the best, the strongest, the one who resists all charms and spells.[^4] When we let ourselves be dragged along by the dangerous feminine magnetism in its extremely varied forms, including the romantic ones (which I consider more dangerous than brute lust), we do not accumulate energy; we only dissipate strength until total weakening and ruin.

According to Samael Aun Weor, every human being is more or less a witch, because he has within himself the I of witchcraft. LÉVI (1855/2001) tells us that vampirization normally occurs among human beings in all social circles, regardless of sex. However, here we are interested only in the way this interesting process takes place from the woman toward the man. While the man in love wastes away day after day, victimized by his own passion, the sly one feels better and better. Some even seem to sense the masculine suffering at a distance in an almost paranormal way. This suffering is not always limited to emotional anguish; there are cases in which the man falls physically ill or suffers accidents. What happens is a synchronous event accompanied by reciprocal, reversed psychosomatic influences: she gets better every day and he gets worse every day. Not a few men die shortly after discovering that their ex-wives have remarried. The betrayed or abandoned husband who gives himself over to alcohol has begun a suicide; the young man who shoots himself in the head after losing his girlfriend has lost his mind. Both have fallen spiritually ill. Within women there may be a <i>femme savant</i>, a <i>femme fatale</i>, a <i>femme fragile</i>, and a <i>femme vamp</i>.

My hypothesis to explain this contradictory behavior, in which the woman provokes the man, challenging him to take dominant stances only to rebel against them immediately afterward, is the following: it is an ancestral mechanism of mate selection among hominid females. By challenging and provoking the male, the female wages a moral struggle with him. If the male defeats her, the feminine unconscious will say that he is a good carrier of the species’ genes. If he loses and takes submissive stances, he will be considered a specimen of inferior category, useful only for functions unrelated to fertilization. By challenging him, she is in truth testing him, and the man who defeats her in this inner war will not be violating her free will but, on the contrary, will be meeting her deepest goals.
