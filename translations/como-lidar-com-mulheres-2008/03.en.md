2. The stages of the work of enchantment on refractory and standoffish women

<!-- seg 03-0002 paragraph h=286b5e1c -->
For the good men who have not yet found a suitable partner and do not know what to do, I will now give a few tips. I do so solely to help the good ones, since women show that they prefer the bad ones[^1]. However, let it be clear that this is not a book about seduction. These tips are only so that the disadvantaged can stand up to the favorites and overtake them in the fierce competition for females.

<!-- seg 03-0003 footnote n=1 h=a9cd2135 -->
It was Eliphas Lévi (1855/2001) who first drew my attention to this fact.

<!-- seg 03-0004 paragraph h=53ae861e -->
The work of enchanting has three major stages. In the first, we have no contact at all with the woman we desire to possess. In the second, we have achieved contact, but our intentions have not been revealed. In the third, our intentions have been revealed. The seduction of strangers belongs to the first stage. Friendship belongs to the second. All the relationships that take place after we declare what we want belong to the third. Let us study the first.

<!-- seg 03-0005 paragraph h=3e4281e9 -->
The guiding principle of the whole work of enchantment is the growing closeness of intimacy mixed with indifference and disinterest.

<!-- seg 03-0006 paragraph h=3cf88f19 -->
Fix your gaze on any woman who is exaggeratedly “beautiful,” stuck-up, snobbish, and not very intelligent. You will see her look away. What is happening in those moments is a rejection, a refusal arising from thoughts in her petulant bimbo brain[^2]. What will she be thinking? It is easy to guess: that you are just one more idiot like any other, that you have nothing interesting about you, because if that were not so you would have some filly by your side and would scorn all the others. So it is a waste of time to go flirting this way, because the ladies who respond will only be the very “ugly” and boring ones[^3] who feel rejected, and not the best ones[^4]. Only desperate women accept harassing men.

<!-- seg 03-0007 footnote n=2 h=502012fa -->
I am thus returning the provocations of Karen Salmansohn (1994), who, throughout her entire book, compares us to dogs that must be trained (she does so explicitly and literally). Despite everything, I am referring only to frivolous women, those who tend to scorn sincere men, and not to the others. I further limit this observation exclusively to the romantic sphere and do not extend it to other spheres.
<!-- QUERY: The original spells the name "Karen Salmanshon"; corrected to Karen Salmansohn, author of "How to Make Your Man Behave in 21 Days or Less Using the Secrets of Professional Dog Trainers" (1994). Check against the bibliography. -->

<!-- seg 03-0008 footnote n=3 h=aa9dd036 -->
According to social conventions. Since beauty does not exist from an objective point of view, let “ugly” be understood as those women who do not consider themselves attractive enough to scorn and disdain the sincere love of the “uninteresting” or “dull” men.

<!-- seg 03-0009 footnote n=4 h=07daa8a2 -->
According to the same social conventions. Modern society overvalues feminine beauty and blames only men for this. But in truth, the women who look in the mirror and consider themselves “beautiful” are often the first to scorn ordinary women and men and to feel superior to them.

<!-- seg 03-0010 paragraph h=3b0d35c8 -->
The most desirable women keep their guard up at all times, and it is no use trying to break through. What must be done is to lead them to lower their guard of their own free will. To allow this opening, you must convey rejection or indifference[^5]. You must find a silent way of telling her, as if you did not want to, that she is uninteresting and that you do not notice her. To do this, it is enough to ignore her presence, avoiding looking at her body and face. But that is not all.

<!-- seg 03-0011 footnote n=5 h=7906a4e1 -->
It is not a matter of simulating, but of acquiring an inner state of true neutrality that will reveal itself in your attitudes.

<!-- seg 03-0012 paragraph h=4c12e6ae -->
Once you have proceeded this way, you will have unsettled her, as you will be able to tell from her gestures and movements (touching her hair, moving around more, fiddling with her clothes, talking loudly to be noticed, etc.). You will begin to be watched, with peripheral or focused vision. Surprise her by greeting her boldly, fearlessly, before she has time to think, looking her in the eyes in an extremely serious way but still with a certain indifference. If you manage to catch her looking at you, she will have no choice but to return your greeting. Contact will have been established. Then, if you want to start a conversation, speak in a commanding tone, with a deep voice, and always be alert to emotional “counterpunches,” little jokes in bad taste, cynicism, etc. If you sense an opening, make your advances, but take care not to go beyond or fall short of what is permitted. If the barrier is still standing, that is, if the woman still remains closed, giving no sign of an opening for an advance, disagree with her opinions, provoke an argument, but do not finish it. Then offer a phone number or e-mail address to continue it, setting a period of waiting.

<!-- seg 03-0013 paragraph h=3eded92d -->
In extreme cases, it is necessary to make a strong impression on her, “horrifying her”[^6] in a calculated way. Do not go “horrifying” her any old way: impress her in the right way, so that the result is not a disaster. A good way of marking her imagination so that she keeps thinking about you for a good while is to own up to being a male chauvinist (an enlightened, conscious, peaceful, and protective one, of course), because your rivals will always pretend[^7] to be feminists in order to please. What matters here is to stand out as a different kind of guy, self-assured, who is not afraid to show his convictions[^8] and who does not need anyone. On this detail, Eliphas Lévi tells us the following:

<!-- seg 03-0014 footnote n=6 h=95f175ba -->
The first author who mentioned this strategy of “horrification,” as far as I remember, was Eliphas Lévi. Several times I thought of replacing this term, because of the confusion it may cause, but I have not yet found a milder equivalent in our language. Its precise meaning here is simply that of contradicting a woman’s convictions about what is beautiful or correct, and never, ever, that of threatening her or exposing her to any real or imaginary danger. This contradiction must always have a final result that is beneficial or harmless to the woman, and never harmful. It is something similar to what boys do by instinct to impress girls at school when they pretend they are going to eat toads, lizards, etc. The girls scream, run... and laugh. In the film “Hitch” (TENNANT, 2001), with Will Smith, calculated and harmless “horrification” is described by the term “shock,” equally liable to misinterpretation, and there is a very interesting example of it.

And therefore they will be neither sincere nor truthful.

<!-- seg 03-0016 footnote n=8 h=7fdbc248 -->
Without exaggeration.

<!-- seg 03-0017 paragraph h=83ef3945 -->
“Whoever wishes to make himself loved (we attribute all these illegitimate maneuvers to man alone, supposing that a woman has no need of them) must, at first, insinuate himself and produce some impression or other on the imagination of the person who is the object of his desire. Let him cause her admiration, astonishment, terror [sic], and even horror[^9] if he has no other resource. But he must, at any price, stand out from ordinary men in that person’s eyes and occupy, willingly or by force, a place in her memories, in her fears, or even in her dreams. The Lovelaces are certainly not the avowed ideal of the Clarissas, but the Clarissas think of them constantly, to censure them, to curse them, to pity their victims, to wish for their conversion and repentance. Soon they will wish to regenerate them through self-denial and forgiveness; next, secret vanity will tell them that it would be charming to win the love of a Lovelace, to love him and resist him; upon saying that she would have liked to love him, she blushes, renounces it a thousand times over, and ends up loving him a thousand times more; later, when the supreme moment comes, she forgets to resist him.” (LÉVI, 1855/2001, p. 337)

<!-- seg 03-0018 footnote n=9 h=6e95e3b3 -->
Lévi is merely describing the process of seduction/conquest as it occurs in real life, regardless of whether or not the perpetrator has scruples, and is not recommending that emotional harm be done to the person seduced. In other words, he is stating that whoever is going to seduce makes an impression on the psyche of the desired person, in a good or bad, harmless or harmful way.

<!-- seg 03-0019 paragraph h=08805ecf -->
“One could say that love, above all in woman, is a true hallucination. For want of any other senseless motive, she will often decide in favor of the absurd. Deceive Gioconda because of a hidden treasure? How horrible! Very well, if it is a horror, why not commit it? It is so pleasant to commit a little horror now and then!” (LÉVI, 1855/2001, p. 338)

<!-- seg 03-0020 paragraph h=1d18edf7 -->
Lévi is referring to a little (and therefore harmless) horror. His explanation helps us understand why the female sex feels so attracted to certain bad and perverse men. They make a strong impression on women, much more than certain good men do. To outdo them, you must master this skill and use it for good, in the right way. If you use it for evil, you will bring bad consequences upon yourself.

<!-- seg 03-0021 paragraph h=cc5f278f -->
Some women tend to seem open at first but, after contact, they fall silent to disconcert us, watching how we get out of this embarrassing situation and having fun at our expense. In this case, be brief and direct[^10] in your remarks, taking the initiative to end the conversation before you end up looking like a fool. If you are on the phone, take the initiative to hang up; if you are talking face to face, take the initiative to end the conversation and walk away without looking back. Put off your advances to another day, teaching her a good lesson. This will impress her. Usually, in the following encounters the lesson takes effect and makes her friendlier... Do not make your advances while her guard is up[^11].

<!-- seg 03-0022 footnote n=10 h=10ad0f31 -->
Without being aggressive or out of control.

<!-- seg 03-0023 footnote n=11 h=31a35b28 -->
That would be harassment. Pushing against a woman’s raised guard is the same as trying to force her will or violate her free will, something detestable whose effect is aversion.

<!-- seg 03-0024 paragraph h=9e758817 -->
Winning a lady has stages that go from the moment when we do not yet know her to the phases in which we have to win her back continually in marriage or in other lasting relationships. In every phase it is necessary to sidestep resistance[^12] and hand her back the consequences of her own decisions. The progression of the phases could be summarized, divided more or less as follows:

<!-- seg 03-0025 footnote n=12 h=dc71c98d -->
By not insisting against it and by seeking alternative paths.

<!-- seg 03-0026 paragraph h=44f12605 -->
1. Subtly greet every interesting woman who passes by you and looks at you. One of them will respond. When a lady looks at you, there is a fraction of a second in which you must greet her. If you wait too long, you will lose the chance. The moment to greet her is the moment when a doubt resulting from the state of surprise hangs in the feminine mind. You can also ignore the beauty’s presence at first, for a good while, and surprise her with a fixed look into her eyes accompanied by an almost imperceptible greeting before she recovers from her surprise.

<!-- seg 03-0027 paragraph h=ad576f0f -->
2. Establish contact as if you did not attach much importance to it.

<!-- seg 03-0028 paragraph h=a7b8ce0e -->
3. Look her steadily in the eyes, showing power.

<!-- seg 03-0029 paragraph h=6cc2555b -->
4. Speak in a protective, commanding tone.

<!-- seg 03-0030 paragraph h=869e84cb -->
5. Speak little; let her talk.

<!-- seg 03-0031 paragraph h=c58e773c -->
6. Move closer to kiss her. If she looks away, stop and try another day. If she does not look away, go on.
