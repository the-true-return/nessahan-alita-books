23. Childishness

<!-- seg 24-0002 paragraph h=fded5453 -->
Human beings, including those of the female sex, retain many characteristics of childhood in adulthood. This is called neoteny:

<!-- seg 24-0003 paragraph h=d71c6b84 -->
“Evolutionary modifications involving the retention of infantile stages are called neoteny. The origin of the chordates is probably the result of a combination of caenogenetic and neotenic processes, since it is generally believed that they arose from a larval echinoderm, and it is almost certain that the shape of the human head originated through the retention of the fetal form. There are in fact many ‘infantile’ human structural characteristics, and they may have originated through neoteny. For example, the retention of the curvature of the skull, the anterior position of the foramen magnum, the flattening of the face, which in turn is always smaller than the braincase, and the absence of hair on the body.” (Harrison and Weiner, 1964/1971, p. 29)
<!-- QUERY: Harrison and Weiner (Human Biology, 1964), translated from the Portuguese edition quoted by the author; the English original is copyrighted, so this is a back-translation. “a tenção” in the source read as “a retenção” (retention). -->

<!-- seg 24-0004 paragraph h=6adf69b3 -->
Let us see how this takes on a feminine behavioral form.

<!-- seg 24-0005 paragraph h=dd928bda -->
Philosophizing on the basis of this scientific precept and comparing it with various observations about human behavior, one can conclude that neoteny is not limited to the physical body but also covers the psyche and behaviors. My point of view, provisional and subject to change as always, is that neoteny is more pronounced in the female sex than in the male, although it is not absent in the latter. The fragile body, the fine features, the high voice, the delicacy of manners, etc., make a woman more like a little girl than a man is like a little boy, and they make her presence very pleasant to us (especially if it is not accompanied by emotional hell-raising). This resemblance to children awakens in us solidarity and the desire to protect them so as not to let them suffer. This in itself is not bad, unless it is turned into an instrument by the sly ones as a weakness through which to take us, weaken us, and manipulate us. Anyone with experience knows that, by talking like little children, women soften a man up and calm him down. It is a strategy that can be used for good, in the case of a woman who is virtuous and honest in her feelings, and for evil, in the case of the selfish manipulator. The frequency with which it is used for evil is not small.

<!-- seg 24-0006 paragraph h=28a83f6b -->
Being considered pleasant because one resembles a child should not be considered an offense, unless the supposedly offended person is prejudiced against children, who are beautiful inside.

<!-- seg 24-0007 paragraph h=670bda70 -->
Women are very similar to children in their habits, their tastes, and even in their fragile physical form. They like candy and chocolates. They constantly play with our feelings. Here there is a subtle difference, because a child does not play with someone else’s sincerity unless it has been taught to, while the sly ones do it with ulterior motives.

<!-- seg 24-0008 paragraph h=167bc109 -->
Try to see them as mischievous children, always staying alert but not giving importance to their silly little games. However, do not forget that they <b>are not really</b> children and can be cunning and even dangerous, in some cases. They are similar to certain tormenting mythical beings that are not evil but do not distinguish things very well either: sacis, caiporas, curupiras, iaras, mermaids, etc. Although they are not really children, they want to be treated as such when it suits them:

I confess that I am skeptical about the possibility that all men might one day act as Cláudia Pacheco suggests above. It would require of them a victory over themselves that would be unique in history.

<!-- seg 24-0012 paragraph h=d198b69a -->
Regarding the childish attitude of feigning naiveté, purity, and saintliness, we must understand that we are all to blame, since we have always tolerated their pretending to be what they are not:

<!-- seg 24-0013 paragraph h=8d8204e6 -->
“Woman has been protected by a [false] ‘halo’ of saintliness in homes and in society. Called the ‘weaker sex,’ defenseless, a symbol of affection, fidelity, and self-denial, she was spared from having to suffer awareness of her pathology, which is immensely serious. This is what finally sank woman. Alienated from her problems, she went into decline day by day, without working on the awareness of her mistakes, which have long gone uncorrected.” (PACHECO, 1987, pp.42-43)

At the point things have reached, there is no alternative but to accept this childishness and give them back the unpleasant consequences, in the hope that one day they will wake up.
