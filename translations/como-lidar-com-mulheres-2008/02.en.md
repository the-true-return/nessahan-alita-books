<!-- QUERY: "vadias" rendered as "tramps", which, like the Portuguese word, means both an idle vagrant and (pejoratively) a loose woman; footnote 2 relies on the "idle person" sense. Alternatives: "loose women", "sluts" (loses the "idle" sense), "good-for-nothings". -->

<!-- seg 02-0010 footnote n=2 h=6a104e23 -->
The word is used here only in the sense of an idle person with nothing to do, as defined by the Aurélio (FERREIRA, 1995) and Michaelis (1995) dictionaries, and not in any other sense. To me, any person who toys with other people’s feelings is a tramp, regardless of sex and of the number of sexual partners. And what else could someone who toys with the sincerity of others be, if not an idler who has nothing more important to do? Here, the word is used more or less like the word “shrew,” and it is also almost a feminine equivalent of the word “cad,” very commonly used to designate men who cheat in love. The term covers those people who commit adultery without their spouse deserving it, who induce a person to fall in love with the sole intention of abandoning him or her afterward, who repay a declaration of sincere love with a slanderous accusation of sexual harassment, etc. This word is not used with the same pejorative sense in all Portuguese-speaking countries, nor does it have only the meaning that popular culture sometimes gives it. A typical example of a “tramp” is the character Teodora in the novel “Love of Salvation,” by Camilo Castelo Branco. In this novel, Teodora, a sly, dissembling, and manipulative woman, takes advantage of the men who love her and drives them to despair and ruin. Afonso, one of her victims, sinks into vice and comes to the brink of suicide, but he is saved from romantic destruction by his cousin, a virtuous and sincere woman.

<!-- seg 02-0015 footnote n=3 h=38bde9ea -->

According to Jung (1996) and Sanford (1986), the feminine <i>animus</i> has a powerful capacity to affect the masculine <i>anima</i>, provoking negative feelings in the man that, in some cases, can lead him to ruin. Hence the importance of a man assimilating and integrating his anima. The anima is the feminine (emotional) part of a man’s psyche, and the animus the masculine (logoic) part of a woman’s psyche (JUNG, 1995 and JUNG, 1996).
