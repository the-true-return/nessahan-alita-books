1. Attractions and repulsions

<!-- seg 02-0002 paragraph h=2913f6a6 -->
On December 29, 2002.

<!-- seg 02-0003 paragraph h=1aa6feb5 -->
Social relations obey magnetic principles, just as inanimate bodies do.

<!-- seg 02-0004 paragraph h=74552661 -->
Human beings establish relations of attraction and repulsion among themselves and with the world: they are attracted by what they like and repelled by what they detest. When they are irresistibly attracted or repelled, universal magnetism is at work.

<!-- seg 02-0005 paragraph h=7f5391c6 -->
Behind magnetic influences lie fascinations. Their quality determines what will be attractive or repellent. The more exposed to fascination we are, the more we will be victimized by circumstances.

<!-- seg 02-0006 paragraph h=6195de08 -->
Magnetic flows form social structures ranging from couples, families, or friendships all the way to humanity as a whole.

<!-- seg 02-0007 paragraph h=b6a9f3c7 -->
Psychic force brings about social aggregations through sympathetic affinity and disaggregations through antipathetic effects. Sympathy arises from the convergence of desires, and antipathy from their divergence.

<!-- seg 02-0008 paragraph h=f26d1164 -->
The direction taken by desire is the flow of the libido. One and the same person has multiple and conflicting libidinal flows. Their main psychological line will be determined by the predominant libidinal flows, which expose them to the danger of manipulation by a cunning enemy experienced in dominating the feelings of others.

<!-- seg 02-0009 paragraph h=7431532d -->
Manipulators intensify sympathy or antipathy by arousing the conscious and, above all, the unconscious desires of their victim, leading the victim to complete dependence, surrender, and submission. The secret of their perverse power is the ingenious strategy of identifying the victim’s passions that will be useful to them, stimulating them, and heightening them. In this way the victim is unconsciously induced to adore, fear, or hate the manipulator.

<!-- seg 02-0010 paragraph h=38011002 -->
Magnetic force is very dangerous. Its power of attraction can be extremely intense and can strike us down. To move it, we need a point of fixity, self-centering, which is obtained through the dissolution of the complexes; this confers on us behavioral freedom and the power to resist the fatal attractions and repulsions of universal magnetism.

<!-- seg 02-0011 paragraph h=0e208e0a -->
It is always advisable, as far as possible, to avoid antipathies, but for that it is necessary to dissolve the egos. Antipathy is generally not favorable to us unless we have an intense dose of sympathy with which to counter it very securely. We must avoid the constellation of antipathies as much as we can.

<!-- seg 02-0012 paragraph h=f55db8f5 -->
When we tamper with the magnetic current, that is, with the interpersonal or intrapersonal libidinal flow, we set off reactions. Foreseeing them is essential if we are not to be struck down.

<!-- seg 02-0013 paragraph h=7cdb5586 -->
The means of determining sympathies and antipathies is observation. Through observation the manipulator discovers what the objects of love and hatred are. Sympathetic affinity is established through the correspondence of attitudes, through the convergence of behaviors. If we act against what someone detests and in favor of what someone loves, we will enter into sympathetic affinity with them.

<!-- seg 02-0014 paragraph h=d4cf9932 -->
To overcome a great antipathy, a greater dose of sympathy is needed. Suppressing an immense hatred or resentment requires the exhaustive application of magnetism in the opposite direction and in proportion to the hostility felt.

<!-- seg 02-0015 paragraph h=fe567cf8 -->
We are highly mechanical beings. We react to events automatically and according to detectable patterns. For us to be induced into actions or into states of mind and feeling, it is enough for the manipulator to know how to provoke them.

<!-- seg 02-0016 paragraph h=d72c25d9 -->
For example, we induce someone who takes pleasure in gratuitous opposition to defend our ideas when we pretend to defend ideas opposed to the ones that are actually ours. We induce a gossip to spread a piece of news when we ask him to keep it hidden on the grounds that it is a great secret. This is how the manipulator acts.

<!-- seg 02-0017 paragraph h=e80b377d -->
The first step in manipulation is to identify the other person’s conditionings. The second step is to discover the trigger of the mechanical action. The third step is the instrumentalization of that conditioning, the discovery of situations in which it is useful to our purposes. Then it is enough to “push the buttons,” and the reactions are set off.

<!-- seg 02-0018 paragraph h=d812ce3d -->
The manipulator takes stock of the behavioral conditionings and of the objects that exert attraction and repulsion on his victim. Then he uses them as circumstances dictate.

<!-- seg 02-0019 paragraph h=a5bde6e1 -->
When the manipulator’s real intentions are perceived, his image suffers wear and tear in the victim’s eyes. To recover it, he will need to act as if the object of his desire were highly uninteresting and then carry on with his enchanting acts.

<!-- seg 02-0020 paragraph h=594929c8 -->
In manipulation one operates by alternation. One does not set force against force but, on the contrary, intensifies and instrumentalizes the existing flows of force. Insistence in a single direction produces a resisting flow of force in the opposite direction. Continual, excessive flattery and affection lead to irritation and weariness. Continual indifference and contempt consolidate coldness and estrangement.

<!-- seg 02-0021 paragraph h=bea0a7ec -->
The manipulator dialectically combines opposites: he adopts enchanting attitudes while at the same time pretending to be uninterested. Then he follows the progress of the process by which his victim is driven mad.

<!-- seg 02-0022 paragraph h=aa735ff4 -->
One can induce internal states in another person through sympathy or antipathy. All our attitudes set off mechanical reactions in others against which they are defenseless. To instrumentalize these reactions, one need only observe the effects of each attitude and discover situations in which they would be desirable.

<!-- seg 02-0023 paragraph h=02fb85a9 -->
People react automatically to circumstances, in a standardized way. They are utterly manipulable through a game of attraction and repulsion that corresponds to the flow of universal magnetism.

<!-- seg 02-0024 paragraph h=59c8a7de -->
The voice and the gaze are powerful tools of enchantment. They induce attitudes in an easily verifiable way.

<!-- seg 02-0025 paragraph h=3be9c3bc -->
To stare down or verbally offend a man of excitable nature is to induce him, without any chance of defense, to create a conflict and fall into negative psychological states.

<!-- seg 02-0026 paragraph h=dad2e69d -->
Sympathy grows closer and more intense when someone takes up another person’s ideas and, through speech, develops and amplifies them as if they were his own. By endorsing his neighbor’s words, he will be fulfilling his neighbor’s will.

<!-- seg 02-0027 paragraph h=89af7076 -->
Continuous contact, but not contact worn thin by one-sided insistence, is essential to the establishment of sympathy or antipathy. Prolonged distance leads to cooling, to neutrality.

<!-- seg 02-0028 paragraph h=ae7c9d6f -->
Around an object of desire or of hatred, an entire magnetic chain involving an infinite number of people can be created.

<!-- seg 02-0029 paragraph h=06d6587d -->
Obviously, desire is contained in hatred in the form of intense impulses to seek distance from, or to cause harm to, the detested object. Wanting to get away from a situation is almost the same as wanting to approach the opposite situation.

<!-- seg 02-0030 paragraph h=fa4173fa -->
In the unconscious levels of the psyche, magnetism has freedom of direction and intensity in its flow. We continually influence one another without realizing it. Herein lies the danger of the skilled manipulator.

<!-- seg 02-0031 paragraph h=842c6afd -->
The skilled manipulator manages to see the hidden part of another’s psyche. He identifies and instrumentalizes weaknesses the victim does not know he has, in order to turn him into a puppet by arousing his frailties and inducing beliefs.

<!-- seg 02-0032 paragraph h=a2b9701b -->
Each person’s patterns of attraction and repulsion have an individual stratum, exclusively their own, and a collective stratum, shared with other people or even with humanity as a whole.

<!-- seg 02-0033 paragraph h=e5f5c4a7 -->
Sympathy takes hold when one person believes that another will help them fulfill their desires. Antipathy takes hold in the opposite situation: when the satisfaction of desire is threatened.

<!-- seg 02-0034 paragraph h=18e739f4 -->
To oppose another person’s satisfaction is to make him our enemy, and to favor it is to make him our friend. To give free rein to the desires of others is to make oneself in some way useful and necessary to them.

<!-- seg 02-0035 paragraph h=162a8171 -->
Against their own desires, ordinary human beings have no resistance at all, because they have not dissolved the ego. No one has ever heard of anyone becoming the enemy of a person for having been helped by that person to satisfy his desires, dreams, longings, etc. It follows, then, that this is a weak point that never closes. This opening to manipulation is used by expert wrongdoers, but it can also be put to use in just cases in which we need to defend ourselves or help someone.

<!-- seg 02-0036 paragraph h=746e69cb -->
Once the passion or desire has been aroused, the person who bears it mobilizes to satisfy it, hurling himself toward the coveted object like a bullet toward its target. It is something absolutely mechanical and irresistible. Controlling this process requires of the manipulator the ability to influence without being influenced, to find uses in other people’s impulses, to accept them just as they manifest, and to know the words and actions that intensify them.

<!-- seg 02-0037 paragraph h=cb603a4e -->
Particularly interesting are the cases in which the manipulated element believes he is deceiving the manipulator by having his desires satisfied. The people most prone to this kind of deception are those who are little evolved, very primitive, and always looking to take advantage at the expense of others. Obviously, the active element must display immense coldness and indifference in order to endure ridicule, scorn, etc., calmly.
