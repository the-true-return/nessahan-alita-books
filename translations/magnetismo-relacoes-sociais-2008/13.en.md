12. The psychological dynamics of enchantment and sorcery

<!-- seg 13-0002 paragraph h=b17272f2 -->
On 07/25/00, 07/31/00, and 11/09/00
<!-- QUERY: Dates converted to the American MM/DD/YY order (original: 25/07/00, 31/07/00, 09/11/00). Alternative: spell out ("July 25, July 31, and November 9, 2000"), as done for the other chapter dates. -->

<!-- seg 13-0003 paragraph h=9b64ef46 -->
What the superstitious call <i>bewitchment</i> corresponds, psychologically, to the fascination of consciousness. Both will be considered here as one and the same thing, not as two analogous things.

<!-- seg 13-0004 paragraph h=299fbc98 -->
Bewitchment is a form of extreme, exacerbated fascination. That is why sorcerers need their victims to believe in their power to kill or to cause illness.

<!-- seg 13-0005 paragraph h=d783a443 -->
An absolutely skeptical person is immune to a sorcerer’s power. If a skeptic is bewitched, this indicates that his skepticism was not absolute, that there was an unconscious wavering.

<!-- seg 13-0006 paragraph h=bba695de -->
The same process can be seen in seduction. A woman is invulnerable to a seducer’s power when she does not believe that he has anything of interest to her and, by extension, the power to attract her.

<!-- seg 13-0007 paragraph h=4ce045ad -->
However, sometimes we believe at first that we are invulnerable to someone’s power and then, in another situation, the appearance of something new and not yet known to us makes our earlier conviction collapse. Then we become vulnerable.

<!-- seg 13-0008 paragraph h=0f2fd5f0 -->
Enchantment requires prior knowledge of the weaknesses of the one who is to be enchanted. It is not possible for someone to be enchanted in a direction contrary to that of his weaknesses.

<!-- seg 13-0009 paragraph h=2807d36a -->
Enchantment occurs only in the direction of preexisting weaknesses, whether conscious or unconscious. It is, therefore, the instrumentalization or exploitation of impulses that already existed: a perverse way of taking advantage of others’ weaknesses and violating their free will.

<!-- seg 13-0010 paragraph h=17aac1b4 -->
The enchanter identifies and arouses the impulses and instincts in his victim until he brings the victim into an altered state of consciousness. The depth of the alteration will depend on each person’s nature and degree of exposure.

<!-- seg 13-0011 paragraph h=f9b81430 -->
We all have weaknesses. These weaknesses correspond to our mad fascination with some things at the expense of others. The objects of this fascination are the instruments of submission to a cunning enemy.

<!-- seg 13-0012 paragraph h=ac168f7b -->
The crimes and killings that plague the world are due to the fascination of consciousness by an object, a target, a goal. It is the greatest psychic danger that can befall us, because it is the weakening of the will taken to the extreme.

<!-- seg 13-0013 paragraph h=eaf49c4e -->
The sorcerer’s power consists in making his victim enter an altered state of consciousness through fear. His rites aim to increase his power to impress and to strike the enemy psychically. This power is intensified when the sorcerer surrenders to possession by highly dense autonomous complexes.

<!-- seg 13-0014 paragraph h=afe9a974 -->
After countless cruelties and torments inflicted on himself and on other people or animals, the sorcerer brings evil into being within himself. Then, possessed, he communicates to his victim what he has done through conscious or unconscious channels. His horrible expression, vocal intonation, bodily gestures, and attitudes strike the victim emotionally and weaken his reason and will. The victim opens himself to these “diabolical” influences and likewise suffers possession by inhuman psychic elements that lay dormant in his unconscious. A psychosomatic shock occurs. The person violently somatizes the fear and dies or falls ill.

<!-- seg 13-0015 paragraph h=01c25485 -->
The first solution for not being vulnerable to witchcraft is not to fear it. That is why devout religious people are invulnerable to it. However, if they are fanatics, they will be vulnerable to the charms and curses of their own religion and can be enchanted, manipulated, and struck by them.

<!-- seg 13-0016 paragraph h=b5af82cb -->
The brutal fascination of consciousness corresponds to the <i>perish of soul</i> studied by Jung. The person loses his habitual “soul” and is possessed by another, demonic “soul,” or rather by a fragmented and autonomous piece of soul that was waiting in the depths of his own psyche to manifest itself. Consciousness is violated by a primitive and grotesque psychic aggregate from the unconscious.

<!-- seg 13-0017 paragraph h=0166b86d -->
There is nothing mystical, fantastic, or magical in this. These are empirically verifiable phenomena.

<!-- seg 13-0018 paragraph h=a87e5283 -->
On a small scale, enchantment is present in our daily lives at every moment.

<!-- seg 13-0019 paragraph h=a54e835c -->
The words we utter, the clothes we wear, and everything we do have the power to provoke in others certain feelings from which they cannot escape. Speech, the subjects and conversations taken up, vocal intonation, attitudes, and glances are means of establishing sympathetic affinity with psychic elements that dwell within the psyche and wait for an opportunity to express themselves.

<!-- seg 13-0020 paragraph h=878bf165 -->
We are all, in one way or another, victims of circumstances. They determine what we will feel and think and even do. This proves that we are bewitched at every moment.

<!-- seg 13-0021 paragraph h=1b411b44 -->
When someone offends us, we normally do not have the power not to feel offended: we are bewitched.

<!-- seg 13-0022 paragraph h=67967fe3 -->
Fascination always occurs with the unconscious collaboration of the victim. The victim suffers it for not knowing how to isolate himself from the other’s hypnotic forces.

<!-- seg 13-0023 paragraph h=f8097698 -->
When two people with desires of the same kind come together, the one with the greater will absorbs and manipulates the will of the weaker one and dominates it. The same phenomenon can be seen in social circles of various kinds. There is always a hierarchy of power in the absorption of other people’s will. These are other modes of enchantment.

<!-- seg 13-0024 paragraph h=cd2d263d -->
Every act is fascinating and hypnotic because it produces effects in another’s psyche. The power of scowls and hostile words to cause discomfort is proof of this.

<!-- seg 13-0025 paragraph h=d531624c -->
There is a hypnotic dynamic in social relations. Men tend to react mechanically to one another. Not infrequently we are forcibly induced to have undesirable emotions. Against our will, we are thrown into certain states of feeling by the attitudes of others.

<!-- seg 13-0026 paragraph h=aa1ec87b -->
Madness and passional exaltation are forms of fascinatory intoxication.

<!-- seg 13-0027 paragraph h=3dd845fc -->
By observing a person, we can know in which direction their libido flows. It is in that direction that the person will fall into madness.

<!-- seg 13-0028 paragraph h=da22e5c3 -->
However, not all enchantment is bad. There are cases in which it is beneficial. E.g.: the religious conversion of wrongdoers.

<!-- seg 13-0029 paragraph h=5a104301 -->
When we work on our psyche, the fascinating effect of external and internal images gradually loses its influence. Then the force that was vampirized in this process, squandered on useless or even dangerous things, can serve us for self-healing and inner self-regeneration.

<!-- seg 13-0030 paragraph h=31c23261 -->
Enchantment, bewitchment, fascination, and hypnosis are various names given to one and the same thing, not to distinct and analogous things.

<!-- seg 13-0031 paragraph h=32820446 -->
When we detest something or someone, we are, without knowing it, negatively fascinated or hypnotized by images linked to those elements.

<!-- seg 13-0032 paragraph h=14169e87 -->
There is an analogical relation between magical procedures and their results. The rites of sorcery and their impact on the victims have a symbolic similarity that careful analysis can demonstrate. This points to the psychic relation that exists between the two. It takes place, for the most part, at unconscious levels. Often the bewitcher and the bewitched are unaware of the complex energetic web that envelops them through words, feelings, thoughts, and attitudes. And it is precisely this that gives magic a supernatural and mystical appearance, for whatever man does not perceive objectively becomes highly attractive to the fanciful imagination. But in truth, magic is a manipulation of natural forces inherent in man.
