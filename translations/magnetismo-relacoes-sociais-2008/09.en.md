8. The self-dominating character of manipulation

<!-- seg 09-0002 paragraph h=42270aae -->
The process of domination is self-propelling. In truth, it is not ultimately the manipulator who dominates the other: the passive element is dominated by its own complexes (defects or egos). By activating the victim’s passional weaknesses, the criminal merely acts as a simple agent who facilitates and intensifies a process that already existed.

<!-- seg 09-0003 paragraph h=19b962ac -->
In manipulation, the charlatan does not impose his whims, longings, and goals against the victim’s will but, on the contrary, gives the victim’s already existing wishes a use. He does not force the victim to go against herself: he throws her headlong into her own desires, dreams, and follies. This is always a crime against the soul and against free will, justified only in special cases of self-defense[^2].

<!-- seg 09-0004 footnote n=2 h=8bfb81ab -->
By self-defense, let it be understood: the act of dismantling the manipulations of an ill-intentioned person.

<!-- seg 09-0005 paragraph h=a853e4ad -->
To find meaning in the tendencies of others requires immense experience in dealing with people and knowledge of the singularity of the passive element. The manipulator discovers, in other people’s tendencies, convergences with his own goals, and does not try to impose, starting from his goals, the tendency to be aroused. However, everything will depend on the objective. If his intention is to destroy or abuse his neighbor, which unfortunately is the most common case, certain specific passions will have to be activated. In cases where the intention is to help, other passions will be aroused. Awakening a love of life in a would-be suicide is a good deed.

<!-- seg 09-0006 paragraph h=95a6fbe4 -->
Given certain specific commands, the emotions necessarily drive people in certain directions. To lead them unconsciously in that same direction, it is enough to know the right commands and apply them. Manipulation depends on the ability to identify the tendencies that, contrary to appearances, lead the passive element toward the objectives. This is the most difficult point. Once the right tendency has been identified, everything flows easily, but the work of identification is not always easy; it requires great experience in dealing with people and knowledge of the specific nature of the person to be manipulated.

<!-- seg 09-0007 paragraph h=85dc2248 -->
The main passions that determine a manipulative process are aversions and desires. Aversions correspond to fears and hatreds; desires correspond to cravings, longings, and dreams. Obviously, all psychic elements are intertwined. Aversions promote antipathy and desires promote sympathy. The victim always tends to believe more readily in what he fears or desires.

<!-- seg 09-0008 paragraph h=62b7e236 -->
Against internal forces, man is defenseless. It is not necessary, therefore, to manipulate him from outside when one knows how to activate the internal elements that will lead him to the desired goal.

<!-- seg 09-0009 paragraph h=9fe0e941 -->
To respect another’s free will is to grant him the right to self-determination, to let him be what he is and do what he wants. It is to respect his desires instead of trying to make them flow the other way. Curiously, when this happens, the person opens up the opportunity to be dominated through himself, unfortunately. We see, then, that human beings are permanently vulnerable and exposed unless they discover and combat their weaknesses.

<!-- seg 09-0010 paragraph h=05f22c3b -->
People can be corrupted through their preexisting bad inclinations. For that, it is enough for the manipulator to kindle those inclinations through words and gestures. Fortunately, by the same route we can regenerate them. To attune oneself to the person one intends to dominate is to be capable of the same attitudes and words to which that person is inclined. The required malleability is achieved only through the death of the egos. Whoever has no passions has no fixed, rigid, and preestablished expectations of others, and can therefore instrumentalize other people’s behavioral tendencies for his own ends. Obviously, if the ego is dead, those ends will be neither selfish nor passional.

<!-- seg 09-0011 paragraph h=b12874fc -->
Unfortunately, it is much easier to arouse another’s passion toward evil than toward good, because evil corresponds to repressed tendencies. Evil corresponds to forbidden desires, which carry an enormous pent-up libidinal charge. Knowledge of the other’s disposition is indispensable and is obtained through observation.

<!-- seg 09-0012 paragraph h=c0b00b38 -->
Rigorous care given to our psychological death makes us immune to the hypnotic and counter-hypnotic effects that these operations set off, allowing us to stand up to charlatans and manipulative scoundrels by returning their influences to them. Without the death of desires, we are victimized by the fascinating forces that we activate or that others try to activate in us.

<!-- seg 09-0013 paragraph h=2a995f52 -->
When dealing with extremely dangerous, complicated, or difficult people, we have to learn to move among their passions. <i>“To place oneself in the same current of thought as a spirit,”</i> as Eliphas Lévi (1855/2001) wrote, is to be able to simulate similarity and convergence of purpose. <i>“To keep oneself morally above it”</i> is to be isolated from that same influence and not be attracted by the same object. In other words: to simulate a behavior while taking care not to be absorbed and dominated by it, to reinforce the other’s ideas without being magnetized by them.

<!-- seg 09-0014 paragraph h=5cf83c5d -->
It all comes down to being inwardly free to allow the passions of others to run their course without being affected or swept away by the current that is created but, on the contrary, sweeping it along.

<!-- seg 09-0015 paragraph h=7ff09662 -->
The wicked need the suffering of the good in order to be satisfied. They make immense sacrifices to harm them and even expose themselves to risks. When they fail to achieve this aim, they suffer emotionally, because the malign energy they have created within themselves finds no receptacle outside and returns to its point of departure, and may even make them ill. This is how the good torment the wicked. Therefore, it is a great advantage for us to be superior to the wicked, and we achieve this when we are impenetrable to fear, hatred, affections, attachments, and all the passions. When we dissolve the egos, we become immune to every spell and enchantment, because we are no longer the opposite, reactive pole that receives magnetism, but rather its emitter. We will be a mirror that reflects and returns exactly what is cast at us. If they cast spells of pain at us (insults, threats, invective, hatred, etc.), we will not suffer, and that pain will return to its point of departure. If they cast enchantments of pleasure at us (ill-intentioned attempts at seduction through flattery, friendly manipulation, sexual insinuation, etc.), we will not become involved or be enchanted, causing the manipulator to fall into frustration and suffer for failing to achieve his purpose. Our aura will repel wicked people.

<!-- seg 09-0016 paragraph h=adc572d6 -->
From a moral point of view, the counter-spell and the counter-enchantment are just, because they are acts of self-defense. There is nothing wrong with defending oneself from a manipulator’s assaults so as to return to him the exact inner consequences of his own attitudes and the resulting poisons that had been distilled and intended for us. The wrong lies in taking the initiative to bewitch or enchant, an act that will always be due to greed and selfish desires. That is why we must forgive, resist hypnotic influences inwardly, and not react to provocations, insults, humiliations, threats, persecutions, etc. However, resisting hypnotic influences does not always mean the absence of action[^3], for there are cases in which it is unthinkable to stand by with folded arms and be complicit with injustice and with the massacre of the innocent and defenseless.

<!-- seg 09-0017 footnote n=3 h=68cc031b -->
But most of the time it means exactly that. The way is the one taught by Gandhi, Buddha, and Christ: non-action and the boycott of wickedness.

<!-- seg 09-0018 paragraph h=c8d87e35 -->
If you are able to go against yourself (your rigid structures of thought and feeling), accepting your manipulator’s goals, points of view, and absurd actions without, however, identifying with them, you will be able to arouse his passions and lead him to destroy himself, like a person struck on the head by the very boomerang he threw.
