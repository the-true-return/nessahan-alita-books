9. The behavioral singularity of the passive element

<!-- seg 10-0002 paragraph h=d8c04ba5 -->
On May 16, 2003

<!-- seg 10-0003 paragraph h=f79c12f1 -->
The manipulator takes care to interact according to each person’s particular temperament, because the tendency to generalize can lead him to fatal errors.

<!-- seg 10-0004 paragraph h=472d9dd1 -->
An attentive observer, he follows our steps and gradually comes to understand how we feel, think, and act, so as to take hold of us through our follies. He contemplates our psychic peculiarities in an attempt to understand our behavioral patterns of action and reaction to events.

<!-- seg 10-0005 paragraph h=89428196 -->
This concern with the behavioral singularity of the element to be manipulated is due to the manipulator’s need not to draw upon himself an unforeseen flow of the magnetic current he intends to unleash. Ignorance of this singularity, the baneful effect of the tendency to generalize, prevents foreknowledge of the totality of the reactions to be unleashed.

<!-- seg 10-0006 paragraph h=3c555b76 -->
The deeper and broader the foreknowledge of the reactions to be unleashed by the manipulator’s actions, the more exactly the results will serve his interests. The power to manipulate another arises in direct proportion to the depth and breadth of one’s knowledge of that person’s mechanical reactions to events. The more the manipulator knows his victim, the more power he will have over the victim.

<!-- seg 10-0007 paragraph h=579f398e -->
But concrete and reliable knowledge is singularizing; hence the caution with generalizations. The skilled manipulator does not act in uncertainty about how the other will react.

<!-- seg 10-0008 paragraph h=919004e5 -->
To disempower and confuse a skilled manipulator, and thus defend ourselves, we have to dissolve the egos. By doing so, we eliminate our mechanical, standardized reactions, leading the observer into error. In studying us, the usurper of wills will draw erroneous conclusions about our patterns of action and reaction. He will then be surprised to notice that we do not react as he had predicted. He will try again several times, but he will always be disconcerted by the absence of reactive patterns.

<!-- seg 10-0009 paragraph h=378c0415 -->
In this way, that is, through the death of the egos, we prevent the manipulator from penetrating our individuality.

<!-- seg 10-0010 paragraph h=ed43110b -->
Observing and interacting with the victim allows the manipulator to identify the victim’s specific and general, primary and secondary fears and desires. Once such weaknesses have been identified, the manipulator will arouse them to unbearable levels so that their baneful consequences make themselves felt.
