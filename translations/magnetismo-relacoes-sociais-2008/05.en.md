4. Manipulation and the instrumentalization of beliefs

<!-- seg 05-0002 paragraph h=1e9b2f07 -->
One of the requirements for provoking passion is to conceal this intention so that the victim’s ego does not react to the manipulation of his mind. Should the victim become aware of the criminal’s real intentions, he will react with indignation at being manipulated and used; he will understand that the manipulator’s false and feigned acts have as their sole aim the control of his behavior.

<!-- seg 05-0003 paragraph h=1fe41ea9 -->
The skilled charlatan makes the victim believe that he is in control of the situation and that he is the one deceiving the charlatan. The victim’s belief in his own autonomy is essential to prevent adverse reactions.

<!-- seg 05-0004 paragraph h=f968cb6b -->
Individual or collective patterns of mechanical reactions obey beliefs. To manipulate beliefs is to manipulate the meanings attributed and, by unmistakable extension, the corresponding reactions. Meanings are made and altered through attitudes. The manipulator will be all the more dangerous the greater his inner freedom, that is, his ability to take opposite attitudes within one and the same situation.

<!-- seg 05-0005 paragraph h=a920f5a7 -->
Because he is able to assume whatever behavior he wishes, the sly operator induces beliefs at will. He acts like a saint so that people will believe he is honest, or like a cad so that they will believe he is dishonest. He acts shy so that they will believe he is a coward, or arrogant so that they will believe he is powerful.

<!-- seg 05-0006 paragraph h=6c84c8f7 -->
Magnetic force skillfully instrumentalized in manipulations of another’s mind is hypnotic. An induced hypnotic state is a state of belief. If the hypnotized person is led to believe he is a dog, he will bark. If he is led to believe that his friend is going to kill him, he will try to defend himself in a life-or-death struggle.

<!-- seg 05-0007 paragraph h=fa014bc8 -->
Beliefs vary in intensity, and they influence conduct in direct proportion to that intensity. We readily believe what we intensely desire or fear.

<!-- seg 05-0008 paragraph h=7f3b2d70 -->
To induce beliefs is to work on the imagination. If I am convinced that so-and-so is a thief, it is because that is how I imagine him.

<!-- seg 05-0009 paragraph h=ab96f9bb -->
Ideological, verbal, or physical combats are decided by the power to induce beliefs. Whoever is induced to believe he is inferior to his opponent will be defeated.

<!-- seg 05-0010 paragraph h=543b74de -->
Beliefs and imaginings, therefore, define the direction in which universal magnetism flows.

<!-- seg 05-0011 paragraph h=6668a29f -->
The one who hates acts according to what he believes can wound his enemy, physically or emotionally, because his intention is to do harm. The one who loves acts according to what he believes can help or protect the beloved. The manipulator weighs his victim’s reactions in advance, with the intention of foreseeing and triggering them. Facing his enemy, he asks himself: in what ways will this person try to harm me if I provoke his hatred? Then he looks for hidden benefits among the possible attempts at harm. If he identifies one, he calculates the probability that the victim will react exactly as foreseen. Then he pushes the buttons. The hater’s beliefs condition his actions toward the hated. The actions of the hated are like psychological buttons that, when pushed, precisely activate certain behaviors. It all comes down to finding the right buttons according to the benefits the manipulator seeks. A miscalculation can be fatal. Hatred is one of the strongest drivers of behavior. Those who eliminate the ego eliminate the buttons.
