5. Woman’s emotional aggression against man
<!-- QUERY: Numbered "5." in the original, although the previous chapter is also "5." and the table of contents lists this one as 6. Kept as in the original (see known_issues). -->

<!-- seg 07-0002 heading h=3ae0b9d9 -->
Why they provoke double feelings

<!-- seg 07-0003 paragraph h=cba18b11 -->
Women alternately provoke happiness and sadness in us because they simultaneously feel love and hatred for man. It is a duplicity of feelings in the personality, similar to schizophrenia.

<!-- seg 07-0004 paragraph h=7fb82ea1 -->
Knowing our emotional mechanisms, they provide well-being, happiness, and pleasure at some moments, but also fury, wrath, resentment, and sadness at others, tearing us apart inside. Those who cannot bear it snap.

<!-- seg 07-0005 heading h=e1a00b35 -->
The female power to attack the feelings

<!-- seg 07-0006 paragraph h=1bb95200 -->
According to common sense, women are supposedly fragile and defenseless beings, while men are supposedly strong and potentially dangerous. There would thus be a need to control the latter by means of various legal mechanisms to contain their “natural aggressiveness.” This idea has been instilled in the masses by the media.

<!-- seg 07-0007 paragraph h=c2a75f4a -->
The media constantly report cases of domestic aggression, implying that men attack women without any motivation and that the latter are their “natural victims.” The whole previous history of emotional violence committed by the woman who was attacked, in the cases where such violence occurred, is carefully avoided and hidden. The paradigm of the defenseless and harmless woman, combined with the idea of the dangerous and cruel male, guides statistics, scientific and journalistic articles, films, soap operas, and even public policies. To doubt it and question it is heresy. Whoever dares to scrutinize it runs the risk of being sent to the stake.

<!-- seg 07-0008 paragraph h=8bea18ce -->
Indeed, the number of women who are physically attacked by the men they live with is high, and measures to curb such aggression are necessary and urgent. But the number of men emotionally attacked by their wives, girlfriends, and fiancées is equally high, and nobody likes to talk about that. Dealing with women’s emotional aggression in love is a taboo; it causes great discomfort. But dealing with men’s aggression in the home is a party, and everyone is always ready to cast one more stone.

<!-- seg 07-0009 paragraph h=bc8a64ae -->
If it is true that men who attack women physically or psychologically must be punished by law, it is no less true that women who attack men in the same way must also be punished, and with equal rigor. And here we reach the point that concerns us: why does nobody consider punishing women for psychological aggression in love?

<!-- seg 07-0010 paragraph h=bf7cec08 -->
While most men attack women physically, women usually attack men emotionally. Although there are many cases of male aggression that are totally unprovoked and cannot be justified under any circumstances, there are also many other cases in which this aggression is the consequence of a long process of emotional torture and torment perpetrated by the woman herself. Emotional pain is real and can reach unbearable levels, to the point of losing one’s sanity entirely. It is felt in the heart, but its qualitative content is hard to grasp and define, since we live in a materialistic world that totally neglects the psychic aspect of life.

<!-- seg 07-0011 paragraph h=8ad0c20e -->
Emotional aggression is dangerous because it can destroy the nervous system and bring on outbursts of madness such as those of “battered man syndrome.” Women are experts at carrying it out because they have much more emotional intelligence than men, who, when they snap, see no other path than the most primitive of all: to attack physically and destroy everything. The power of emotional aggression is usually neglected, even though its effects are constantly felt. There is no man who has not experienced it.

<!-- seg 07-0012 paragraph h=23ea49f1 -->
Among the forms of emotional aggression perpetrated by women against men, we can mention: marrying and refusing to fulfill the obligations of a wife; lying; behaving in a way that leaves her fidelity in doubt; going out without accounting for herself; devoting herself more to her girlfriends and to work than to the home and her husband; disparaging her husband in front of others; not allowing her ex-husband to see the children; committing adultery; frequently traveling without her husband; promising and refusing sex; demanding sexual fidelity from the man but refusing to satisfy him sexually; trading her companion for other people; faking suicide attempts; flirting with other men and denying it despite the evidence; filling her husband’s head with doubts; spending all his money on useless things; doing exactly what he does not want in order to exasperate him; pushing him away when he hugs her; rejecting him as “clingy” after having demanded or allowed him to fall in love; inducing him to fall in love in order to turn him into a slave; being cold and distant; behaving in a dissembling way to confuse him; breaking off the relationship but behaving in a way that gives hope of a return; etc. Although all this seems like little to women, it is not seen that way from the male point of view. From the male point of view, this set of torments constitutes something serious and truly gives rise to violent nervous crises. Regardless of whether we like it or not, or whether we think men are childish or not for attaching importance to these aspects of romantic life, the fact is that what is important to a man seems like nonsense to a woman and vice versa. Even so, she manages to wound him unerringly in his feelings.

<!-- seg 07-0013 paragraph h=ea477cef -->
The range of acts that constitute emotional violence in the romantic relationship is so large that we would have to fill an entire book to describe them all in detail. Roughly speaking, however, we can sum it up in three categories and say that what most wounds and enrages a man in the romantic relationship is: the indefiniteness, the dissimulation, and the contradictions of female behavior.

<!-- seg 07-0014 paragraph h=1b02a6f6 -->
Human beings need certainties to remain emotionally healthy, especially in the field of love. Indefinite, dissembling, and contradictory behaviors steal certainty and cause emotional and mental stress, with results that are highly harmful to the couple, but especially to the man, since there are no laws that protect him against the psychological aggression of his companion.

<!-- seg 07-0015 paragraph h=dc8216f7 -->
The certainties a man needs to remain healthy are, mainly, the certainty of his partner’s fidelity and of being loved in return by her, in the romantic and sexual field. The doubts that torment him are the suspicion of being deceived and cheated in this field. For a man, sex and love, although separate, are extremely important, and he cannot bear betrayal in either of the two fields. The mere idea that he might be being traded in is already sheer hell. The reason for this is that the contemporary human male is as territorial as in the time of the caves and will never stop being so, because instincts cannot be suppressed or denied. Woman also has caveman instincts, but nobody wants to admit it so as not to disturb the myth of the harmless and defenseless goddess. Among the various forms of emotional aggression that can be committed against men by their women, the most destructive are those that fall within the scope of marital fidelity, and this should be taken much more seriously by psychiatrists and psychologists.

<!-- seg 07-0016 paragraph h=c59f1578 -->
Generally, the end of the marriage and separation strike a man violently in the heart, not least because it is almost always he who leaves the house and does not keep the family. The separation, the end of the family, and the ex-wife’s union with the lover she hid for many years are the powder keg. The adulteress’s disdain is the fuse. The result is a psychotic break.

<!-- seg 07-0017 paragraph h=56b27ea1 -->
The psychopath who kidnaps his own children and his wife, murders them, and then commits suicide is not born overnight. He takes shape over several years of exposure to many forms of violence at the level of the feelings. If it were a simple case of “bad character” or cruel crookery, he would never commit suicide; he would simply flee far away. The suicide indicates that the wretch has gone mad and wants to be with his family forever, in the other world.

<!-- seg 07-0018 paragraph h=21a6d2e8 -->
It is right to punish husbands who physically attack their wives. But it is not right to leave unpunished the wives who emotionally attack their husbands. If we want to put an end to domestic violence between couples, we cannot allow any of its causes to remain, and the emotional aggression perpetrated by women is one of those causes.
