19. Freeing the soul

<!-- seg 20-0002 heading h=725afe2d -->
Changing destiny

<!-- seg 20-0003 paragraph h=c0559195 -->
It is the right of every man[^13] to transform himself into someone different from what he has always been. Every man has the right to modify himself and thus to change his life. We have the right to think today in a way totally different from, and even contrary to, the way we thought yesterday. We have the right to adopt new conceptions, new ideologies, new stances, new attitudes, new conduct and new behaviors. To remain petrified in the past, static, is not to evolve. Whoever perfects himself necessarily changes; he transforms himself into another. That is why we have to adopt a continuous construction of our conceptions.

<!-- seg 20-0004 footnote n=13 h=448a592b -->
In this chapter, I am using the word “man” generically, also including woman under this term. I would like to point out that the use of this generic expression often harms the male sex instead of benefiting it. Consider, for example, the cases in which it is said that “man destroys nature,” “man is a wolf to man,” “cursed is the man who trusts in man,” etc. In all these cases the generic expression is used to the detriment of the male side.

<!-- seg 20-0005 paragraph h=a4e7bb44 -->
Throughout our existence, we suffer a great deal emotionally. Our existential suffering has as its fundamental origin, in my view, the imprisonment of the soul in animal impulses. Such impulses are distortions of pure natural instincts that become deformed by the abstract mind when we reach the humanoid state. If they once served to guide the soul and protect the physical body and the species, now they enslave it and drag it like leaves in the wind, toward destruction. The suffering that originates from unsatisfied desire, from the swindles and selfish wiles of the person we love, from romantic passion, from betrayal, from hatred, etc. has its origin in animal impulses. We are animals in the process of humanization, which is why we have to transcend the impulses. Allowing blind impulses to guide us is to throw oneself headfirst over the precipice. We should not “smother” the impulses; we should transcend them.

<!-- seg 20-0006 paragraph h=e81494fe -->
Life is a network of paths, with many branches. Desires draw us along these roads and build our destiny. Every attitude has multiple consequences that unfold over time. What we are today results from choices made in the past. Since desire disregards free will, our destiny is woven unconsciously to a great extent.

<!-- seg 20-0007 paragraph h=5094f932 -->
When we fall into a given situation, we do so as a result of having set off in that direction earlier. Had we set off in other directions, we would now be in other situations (the butterfly effect).

<!-- seg 20-0008 paragraph h=50959d0d -->
It is always advisable to pay attention to the possible future consequences of our great and small acts, in order to avoid whatever might harm us or others. In this way we avoid acquiring karmic debts.

<!-- seg 20-0009 paragraph h=0c119d38 -->
If one person fights with another, this is because both set off down this path in the recent or distant past. Had they in no way entered the road of conflict in the past, they would not be in conflict now; the conflict would not be possible.

<!-- seg 20-0010 paragraph h=bd2304f2 -->
We can avoid disagreements with our partner, with family members or friends by getting ahead of them and avoiding them. Before a conflict sets in, there are many signs that betray the fact that we are heading down that path. It seems better to me to prevent than to remedy, although we also need to know the means of remedying.

<!-- seg 20-0011 paragraph h=47e394a4 -->
To change the course of our life, we have to begin by avoiding mistakes before they arise and by turning away from destructive paths. This applies to great and small things. If I cannot refrain from a glass of alcohol when I am in a bar, then I should not go into the bar. And if I cannot avoid going into a bar when I meet a friend, then I should not meet this friend, at least until the day I am cured of this vice and can then influence him to go with me to other, better places.

<!-- seg 20-0012 paragraph h=cc45291d -->
Any destructive act or situation can be analyzed in search of its origins. A man may drink because he meets a friend, and he may meet a friend whenever he goes to a certain place. But he may go to that place whenever he wants something specific, which apparently has nothing to do with the friend or with the vice. The chain of events that follow one another in time in a causal relationship has no definite limit.

<!-- seg 20-0013 paragraph h=fda567d3 -->
The death of details offers us the opportunity to divert completely the existential course programmed by the egos according to the law of recurrence, launching us onto a path whose direction, from then on, will be consciously determined to a great extent.
<!-- QUERY: "morte dos detalhes" → "death of details" and "lei de recorrência" → "law of recurrence" (Gnostic terms from Samael Aun Weor, as usually rendered in English). Alternative for the first: "death of the small details." -->

<!-- seg 20-0014 paragraph h=5c3c2b95 -->
Small acts, apparently harmless, can have serious undesirable consequences. Hence the importance of keeping watch over ourselves.

<!-- seg 20-0015 paragraph h=d2ba5ab8 -->
The manifestation of a small detail opens the way for the manifestation of other defects, ever greater and more serious. It is much easier to avoid the first slip than to get out of the mud once we are stuck up to our necks. But if we are already stuck in the mud, and this is the case with most of us, we have to avoid sinking still deeper and also avoid treading this path at the next opportunity, which arises every day.

<!-- seg 20-0016 paragraph h=7b78a979 -->
Those who tread this path to “total sinking in the mud” every day reinforce the path and make it wider and wider. But they also have, every day, the opportunity not to start treading it, and therein lies the key to their salvation. They must discover the before of the before, where the point lies at which they began to tread it, get ahead of it a little more still and stay there. At that starting point of the wrongful act lies an important detail to be watched.

<!-- seg 20-0017 paragraph h=62a507cd -->
A sequence of acts that, one after another, bring us closer and closer to a catastrophe has its beginning in something apparently harmless. There lies the starting point on which we must work.

<!-- seg 20-0018 paragraph h=c04c6668 -->
To free ourselves from the clutches of a destructive behavior, we have to know its causes. On the three-dimensional level of the physical world, such causes are rooted in the acts, thoughts and situations that preceded the behavior in time and that should have been avoided. Had they been avoided, we would not have arrived at the present situation.

<!-- seg 20-0019 paragraph h=01f49ffd -->
What matters is to begin a new life. And one cannot begin a new life while preserving the elements that gave rise to the old, undesirable life. To begin a new life, we have to change whatever is giving rise to the problems in our present life. In some cases, it is worth moving house, forming closer friendships with people different from those we associated with in giving rise to the problems, doing new things and ceasing to do the old things that have always resulted, through an often long chain of events, in catastrophic situations for us or for our fellow human beings.

<!-- seg 20-0020 paragraph h=1df335fa -->
In the case of love, I believe we should not become involved with a person who makes our life worse. We should become involved with someone who allows us to make her life better, or else look for another person for a romantic relationship. And if the desire to become involved with complicated people is very strong, we have to discover where the causes of this desire lie (the why before the why that came before the why...). We have to go back as far as we are able.

<!-- seg 20-0021 heading h=237234fd -->
Not feeding the defects

<!-- seg 20-0022 paragraph h=8ea54cd7 -->
It is fundamental to cut off the food of the defects we aim to dissolve. The food is cut off by avoiding the situations that favor them. It is contradictory to allow oneself to fall into situations favorable to the desires while aiming to eliminate them. The pretext of throwing oneself into such situations in order to observe the Ego is a trap the Ego sets in order to survive, for it is a mistaken use of the psychological gymnasium. Throwing oneself into the gymnasium to “enjoy” the flavor of events, instead of discovering these fascinating enjoyments so as to separate ourselves from them, leads us to an even greater enslavement to the “I’s.”
<!-- QUERY: "ginásio psicológico" → "psychological gymnasium" (Samael Aun Weor's term for daily life as a field for self-observation, as usually rendered in English). -->

<!-- seg 20-0023 paragraph h=87ef864e -->
Therefore, the same psychological gymnasium that serves for the discovery and liberation of the soul can serve for its imprisonment if it is taken the wrong way.

<!-- seg 20-0024 paragraph h=7e115ef6 -->
A person addicted to pornographic films will not manage to free himself from them if he goes on watching them on the pretext of “studying lust.” In truth, he will not be studying anything, because he will be identified. A drug addict will not manage to free himself from the addiction if he goes on meeting his addict friends on the pretext of “studying his own addiction.” What should they do? Discover the psychic elements that start these self-destructive processes, eliminate them and completely change the course of their paths. The pornography addict should no longer go near newsstands or movie theaters; the drug addict should move to another city and make friends with other people. Thus, by avoiding the causes, we free ourselves from the consequences.

<!-- seg 20-0025 paragraph h=bae54fb2 -->
It is of no use at all to fight against the defects, observe the thoughts, etc. if we are strengthening them at every moment through unsuspected channels.

<!-- seg 20-0026 paragraph h=bf2cf3b3 -->
In short, we cut off the food of the defects and bring about their withering when we grow accustomed to reacting immediately to their faint approach, not allowing them to settle in. Their approach in any center is quickly detected by one who is in a state of alertness.

<!-- seg 20-0027 heading h=7d4dd798 -->
Moments to take advantage of

<!-- seg 20-0028 paragraph h=5abd60cd -->
There are moments when the passions and desires cool down and leave us in peace, temporarily. These are moments when we should work intensely on these psychic elements to weaken them, driving from the mind all related thoughts and keeping away from situations that usually lead us to rock bottom. Before the undesirable moments return, that is, before the desires and passions we are fighting against trouble us again, we have to tread other paths that lead us in different directions and avoid the same addictive paths along which we always end up stuck up to our necks.

<!-- seg 20-0029 paragraph h=ed059266 -->
The moments of cooling usually follow the satisfaction of desires (which does not mean that we should give ourselves over to satisfaction in order to bring them about). These are moments when it really comes to seem that we will no longer be tormented by the undesirable impulses again and that we are definitively satisfied, forever. However, the error of such an idea is not long in revealing itself: after a lapse of time, short or long, dissatisfaction returns and desire presses once more for satisfaction. Here there is an important point to be considered.

<!-- seg 20-0030 paragraph h=54e1f6e8 -->
It so happens that the return of desire does not take place abruptly. Before we are shaken again by the impulse itself, small reminiscences arise, small memories, thoughts or acts that mark the beginning of the manifestation that will soon be repeated. Such subtle, almost imperceptible reminiscences are not difficult to disintegrate, since they have not yet gathered strength. If we stay vigilant against them long before they appear, getting ahead of the manifestation, we can detect them at the exact moment they begin to sprout and react immediately. How should we react? By breaking the identification and praying for the disintegration of that impulse, which is still subtle at this level of manifestation[^14]. The fact that the manifestation is still weak makes dissolution immensely easier, which is why it is supremely important not to allow it to grow strong and grow, which happens quickly after a few seconds of negligence. All it takes is for us to let a single wrongful thought settle in, and soon we will be unable to rid ourselves of its baleful results.

<!-- seg 20-0031 footnote n=14 h=4e323a52 -->
I strongly recommend that the reader go deeper into this subject in the Gnostic literature.

<!-- seg 20-0032 paragraph h=7e62fcc0 -->
By the path described above, we can free our soul from dangerous sexual desires, from violent romantic passions, from destructive jealousy, from wrath, from tragic hatred and from all the negative and harmful impulses that make us or our neighbor sick. Without freeing the soul from animal impulses, it is not possible to transcend the hells of the love relationship, nor other related psychological hells. Whoever does not emancipate his soul remains bound to inner sufferings, among them suffering in love.

<!-- seg 20-0033 heading h=8df661ab -->
The mind is a problem

<!-- seg 20-0034 paragraph h=01276cde -->
The mind is the great enemy of the human being. We suffer because we think. The passions grow strong and dominate us because we think. Thinking, remembering and imagining are ways of strengthening desire. Desire has the mind as its foundation. If we do not remember, we do not desire. We desire because we think. That is why the quietness of the mind is our continual and principal concern.

<!-- seg 20-0035 paragraph h=383dbdbf -->
But there are insistent thoughts that come back, and back, and back, bringing their desires with them. My explanation for this is the following: such thoughts are the secondary product of hidden primary thoughts. In other words: thoughts that we do not see or to which we give no importance (because they seem harmless) open the way for other thoughts, whose harmful character bothers and worries us. Before a thought insists, it is preceded by other precursor thoughts. Innocent thoughts make room in our mind for worse thoughts. Hence the importance of keeping watch.

<!-- seg 20-0036 paragraph h=f61a1c82 -->
The liberation of the soul lies in forgetting.

<!-- seg 20-0037 paragraph h=e584d13b -->
By watching the mind, we get ahead of bad thoughts and, consequently, of the desires they bring.

<!-- seg 20-0038 heading h=1f75a01c -->
The harm done by desires

<!-- seg 20-0039 paragraph h=2dca2022 -->
We have seen, on television and in the newspapers, how desires lead people to disgrace. And the more we occupy ourselves with these same desires, the more we reinforce them. The more we struggle, the worse. The more we give in, equally the worse. The solution lies in a third path, which is that of the death of the Ego (observation without identification, accompanied by understanding and prayer).

<!-- seg 20-0040 paragraph h=959d814c -->
It is a mistake to try to contain or repress our own attitudes while at the same time reinforcing them with thoughts and memories. To dissolve passion, we have to discover in ourselves everything related to it and separate ourselves from all of it.

<!-- seg 20-0041 heading h=428f83e5 -->
A cause of failure

<!-- seg 20-0042 paragraph h=aa201018 -->
One of the reasons why we fail to overcome our defects is the interruption of vigilance and/or of devotion.

<!-- seg 20-0043 paragraph h=a062b76d -->
There are moments when we are absorbed by the worldly concerns of everyday life and forget our complete dedication to the Spirit. These are moments when we are distracted by commitments that seem very important to us but that, when all is said and done, are of no use at all.

<!-- seg 20-0044 paragraph h=880aca20 -->
The moments of negligence are also called the “spiritual night.” The I’s subtly grow stronger in such dark phases and suddenly burst out with full force, always dragging us a little further toward rock bottom. In the moments of night, we have the impression that everything is fine and that we do not need to attend to vigilance and prayer. Then the baleful results are not long in coming.

<!-- seg 20-0045 paragraph h=840b5f5d -->
The solution to such a problem consists in never allowing vigilance and prayer to cool, taking special care in the moments when we feel safe.
