4. Correct understanding of the theory

<!-- seg 05-0002 paragraph h=0b117d95 -->
The theory I defend in my books requires reading all the volumes to be correctly understood. The books are written in sequence, each one seeking to fill gaps and correct deficiencies in those that precede it. A partial reading will result in a deficient interpretation of their contents.

<!-- seg 05-0003 paragraph h=7f214533 -->
Since I value the continuous construction of knowledge, should there be an absolute (and not merely apparent) incompatibility of ideas between one work and another, the idea defended in the more recent work should prevail. I base myself on theoretical study and on experience, and neither of them is static.

<!-- seg 05-0004 paragraph h=46d39781 -->
Those who take what I write as finished ideas have simply understood nothing. I am totally opposed to dogmatisms. It never hurts to remember that I propose only a line of thought for reflection. I am no different from anyone else, and what I write could never replace the guidance of a true master (someone who has attained a state of turya, or awakened consciousness, as they say in India). That is why I do not like being called “master.” I am only a collaborator, a helper interested in these subjects who likes to share his point of view. I know that people need masters, I understand this need, but I cannot fill it. People feel the lack of a master keenly, and this is understandable. But they have already come many times and left their guidance. It is up to us to apply it. True masters were Buddha, Jesus, Muhammad and many others who came and who surely are still out there today. A master is a man inspired by God, so inspired that at times his soul merges with Him.
