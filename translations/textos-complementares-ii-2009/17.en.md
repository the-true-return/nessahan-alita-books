16. Some (in)definitions and (un)certainties to be established

<!-- seg 17-0002 paragraph h=0b1c0c32 -->
In love there are certain things that must be made very clear and, if the woman is one of those who like to abuse our sincerity, others that must be kept very muddled. We men hardly ever understand this properly. Women seem to be born knowing it...

<!-- seg 17-0003 paragraph h=2945f301 -->
Abandonment may result from the certainty that it is impossible to hook us or from the certainty that they have already hooked us completely. In the first case, it is giving up out of fear of defeat. In the second, it is a triumphal withdrawal. The thing is that disinterest usually strikes them when they acquire the following certainty: <i>“He is firmly hooked on me; I don’t need to worry anymore”</i>.

<!-- seg 17-0004 paragraph h=a7a46e96 -->
The woman attaches herself to the man and worries only as long as doubt is preserved. Doubt is preserved by indefinite male behavior.

<!-- seg 17-0005 paragraph h=ac9ea8f7 -->
Doubt about what is feared can cause more emotional suffering than the certainty of what is undesirable.

<!-- seg 17-0006 paragraph h=f822c687 -->
If our partner is difficult in love, we must correctly combine ambiguous behavior with definite attitudes. We do this in the following way: 1) offering indefiniteness (doubts) regarding what we feel; 2) offering definitions (certainties) regarding the attractions we have (protection, security, self-confidence, leadership, etc.) and also regarding the consequences of swindles and betrayals.

<!-- seg 17-0007 paragraph h=d22630bc -->
What provides certainty is clear behavior. What provides doubt is vague behavior that leaves “something in the air” and gives the feeling that there is “something hidden.” Women are curious beings who cannot live without mystery. It is as if mystery gave them meaning. When the mystery ends, interest usually ceases to exist as well. Those who show themselves completely, moved by sincerity, do not reap good fruits in love. But neither can we be insincere, so as not to justify the insincerity of others. As a solution to this philosophical dilemma, I propose: that we be sincere up to the point where sincerity does not provoke disinterest and, beyond this point, not be insincere, but only mysterious.
