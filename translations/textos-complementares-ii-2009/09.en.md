8. A war for a prize

<!-- seg 09-0002 paragraph h=420a974a -->
It is a common tendency, in the war of passion, for the feeling of triumph to belong to the one who got what he or she longed for from the start. It is not the only existing form of victory, but it is one of the possible forms.

<!-- seg 09-0003 paragraph h=b90f8fa6 -->
In this case, the man longs to satisfy his impulse through the sexual act, and the woman longs to satisfy her desire to preserve male interest (the desire for continuity). The man will only feel satisfied if he completes the act, and the woman will only feel (temporarily!) satisfied as long as she receives constant demonstrations of interest from the man. After satisfaction, the man needs some time for his interest in the woman to be renewed. Therefore, we can say that male eroticism is discontinuous (it alternates in a cycle of interest and disinterest), while female eroticism is continuous, for the woman never stops wanting to be desired; her desire to be the object of the man’s interest never cools. The author who drew my attention to this detail was Francesco Alberoni.

<!-- seg 09-0004 paragraph h=646baa3c -->
Going beyond Alberoni, however, I see an incompatibility between the male and female goals: they are mutually exclusive. It is from this incompatibility that the war of passion arises, made up of battles of love that good and sincere men usually lose.

<!-- seg 09-0005 paragraph h=323ad94d -->
It is not possible for both parties to come out victorious: if one wins, the other will lose. From the point of view from which I am now approaching this emotional conflict, the act of giving herself sexually is seen by the woman as a defeat, hence the countless compensatory demands. On the other hand, the same event is seen by the man as a victory, hence his sometimes insane insistence on getting the “final prize.”

<!-- seg 09-0006 paragraph h=590a8cd2 -->
She will feel triumphant as long as she is able to resist him, as long as she does not give what he wants so much and is able to keep the flame of that desire burning. She will feel defeated if she satisfies him, because in that case the desire will end. But she will also feel defeated if she falls into the opposite extreme, acting so consistently that the man’s feelings become resolved until every shadow of interest ends, through total giving up.

<!-- seg 09-0007 paragraph h=c764adb4 -->
In the course of this war, both want to win; no one wants to lose. But the resources used are not, not by a long shot, necessarily honest. The weapons are the infernal and accursed little games. And women are the ones who master them best.

<!-- seg 09-0008 paragraph h=e6fdc62a -->
The suffering that comes from passion is the suffering of defeat. We suffer because we did not win, because we feel defeated, we feel we are on the losing end. When we come out victorious, the suffering is transferred to the other party. But there is a better way, not so vile, to escape this suffering: to defeat passion within ourselves.

<!-- seg 09-0009 paragraph h=45c63f19 -->
The vagina is a kind of prize. If the man is victorious, the prize will be his. If the woman is victorious, the prize will stay with her. The war is to decide who will keep the trophy. The wise and experienced man does not fight desperately for the trophy. He knows that, if he does, he will be falling straight into the trap, diving headfirst into defeat. Instead, he dismantles the wiles and puts an end to the possibilities of conflict. The experienced man transcends the war of passion; he leaves it behind.

<!-- seg 09-0010 paragraph h=d64feb94 -->
As long as we persist, insist and press, by whatever path, to satisfy our desire, we are granting victory to the woman, because we are giving her the chance to resist. We only rob her of the triumph when we ally ourselves with her resistance, pushing her in the same direction in which she resists. Then we use her own strength to dismantle her wile, leading her, by her own efforts, to an unequivocal stance free of ambiguities. This is how we rob the female party of the triumph and dissolve the conflict.

<!-- seg 09-0011 paragraph h=7f627aab -->
What matters, in the battles of love, is to resolve our own feelings and not to force the other person to do what she does not want to. And feelings are resolved when doubts dissipate completely. You will not manage to wrest the necessary answers from her through disagreement, but only through agreement. The truths they stubbornly hide cannot be wrested out by opposition, but they can be brought to light when we agree with and ally ourselves to their stubborn resistance, carrying it even further than the sly ones had planned. Accept the resistance and carry it through to its ultimate consequences, and the truth will appear[^9]. Then the doubts will end and the situation will be resolved.

<!-- seg 09-0012 footnote n=9 h=9b0bac2a -->
She will not say explicitly what you want to find out and will not confess anything, but what was hidden behind the dissimulation will be revealed by her attitudes.

<!-- seg 09-0013 paragraph h=7b0cbe56 -->
I believe it is a serious mistake to resist in one direction while the woman resists in the opposite direction. This tug-of-war gives them victory.

<!-- seg 09-0014 paragraph h=2c435963 -->
Of course, the principle of non-resistance, which I have described in this chapter, must, like all the others, be correctly dosed and blended with opposite principles, as circumstances present themselves. There may be situations in which this principle is not the most advisable, or is even harmful. As a rule, it works well when the sly ones pretend not to want something (a date, for example) but, deep down, want us to insist and insist endlessly, to inflate their egos.
