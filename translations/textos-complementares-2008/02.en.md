Animal sexuality

<!-- seg 02-0049 paragraph h=6bef01f1 -->
Observing the males of various species, I found that, just like us, they become desperate for lack of sex and lose their sense of reality. When deprived of females, dogs try to copulate with legs and trees, turtles climb on top of melons, bulls jump barbed-wire fences in search of a distant cow in heat, horses and donkeys chase people with an erect penis, and roosters become highly aggressive. The same does not seem to occur with their females, which remain calm and tranquil for a long time. When excessive abstinence finally makes itself felt in them, it is solely in the form of nervousness and “short fuses” (the animal attacks other animals or its keeper more easily, for example), but not as attempts to force copulation with beings of another species or with physical objects[^4]. This male peculiarity ought to be taken into consideration when judging the infidelity and promiscuity of the human male. There is not much difference, in this respect, between us and the other animals.

<!-- seg 02-0050 footnote n=4 h=a7103035 -->
This fact leads me to suppose that females are impelled to mate by motives totally different from those that impel males, that is, the pure and simple desire to physically feel the sexual act.

<!-- seg 02-0051 paragraph h=36727635 -->

Pessimism

<!-- seg 02-0099 paragraph h=29564c45 -->
People do not understand Schopenhauerian pessimism. They suppose it to be a kind of eternal lamentation associated with the masochistic cult of sadness when, in truth, it is a realistic stance in which one gives up seeking happiness in life because one understands that it can only be found in death. By life read “matter” (body), and by death read “spirit” (soul). When the desire to live dies, sadness dies too, and happiness is attained.

For further study, I suggest that the reader research the subject within contemporary Gnosticism.

Murphy’s law

<!-- seg 02-0108 paragraph h=86289827 -->
Love is governed by a pessimistic law: the people who fall in love with us and pursue us are precisely those we do not want, while those we ardently desire usually avoid us. It is Murphy’s law in action. This law was already known to the ancient sages long before Murphy formulated it.

<!-- seg 02-0109 heading h=61e0395a -->
The law of attraction

<!-- seg 02-0110 paragraph h=ac320505 -->
Murphy’s law is complemented by the law of attraction. When we ardently desire someone, we unconsciously believe that that person is inaccessible to us or far beyond our reach. This unconscious belief governs the unfolding of events and leads us to undesirable and unlucky results, because “reality” is constructed by the mind, which selects parts of an absolute and infinite whole to construct its own private world. If we modify our unconscious beliefs, which is not easy, we also modify reality. Each belief that we carry within corresponds to a mental form, to a pole, to a specific viewpoint, to a desire and, therefore, to a distinct “I.” As a matter of logic, when we dissolve a desire, its corresponding unconscious self-sabotaging beliefs dissolve along with it. That is how we transcend Murphy’s Law.

<!-- seg 02-0111 paragraph h=77dd41ea -->
The Law of Attraction holds that if we become deeply convinced of something, it becomes reality. Well then, “becoming deeply convinced of something” means convincing the unconscious, overcoming resistances and skepticisms that we have but whose existence is unknown to us. That is how witchcraft and the magical rites of good and evil work. The laws of universal magnetism govern the attraction between male and female and all the other existing forms of attraction.
