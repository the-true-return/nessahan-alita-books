3. The game of blockages and hopes

<!-- seg 04-0002 paragraph h=925a4202 -->
At the same time as she shows herself attractive, receptive, and friendly, in order to awaken desire in the man and preserve his hopes, the sly one imposes difficulties and obstacles to his approach. She creates an ambiguous and incomprehensible situation. It seems that she wants something, but it also seems that she wants nothing. In the end, the man himself ends up believing that all the interest and initiative came from him alone and that, on the woman’s part, there was no participation whatsoever. When the manipulation is skillfully executed, it always ends up looking as though he wants it and she does not. Then, omitting her own participation, the sly one tells everyone: <i>“Ugh! This annoying guy just won’t get off my back!”</i>

<!-- seg 04-0003 paragraph h=e7e2812b -->
It is a problem with two poles. On one side are the attractive behaviors that create, allow, and preserve our hopes and, on the other, the resistant behaviors that block and prevent our approach. How can it be solved?

<!-- seg 04-0004 paragraph h=befe46c1 -->
One possible path is for us to be even more absurd and illogical, insisting in the direction in which the woman least expects us to. What direction is that? The direction of the negative pole. It is a surprising insistence because most men insist on the opposite pole. Let us look at this more closely.

<!-- seg 04-0005 paragraph h=4d938ec9 -->
Instead of insisting that the woman give in and pressuring her to say that she feels attracted, filling her with questions, arguing, etc., it is more advisable, in order to wrest a definition from her, to insist in the opposite direction, asking her to confirm that the blockages and resistances really correspond to her true interest (<i>“Are you sure you only want to be my friend?”, “Are you really sure you don’t want to meet up with me?”</i>). As a rule, the man tries to apply pressure in order to obtain the desirable confirmations that interest him, and not the uninteresting, dreaded, and undesirable confirmations. And here lies our mistake: we fear that the sly one will confirm exactly what we do not want.

<!-- seg 04-0006 paragraph h=a1b20cd3 -->
If, instead of “pointing a pistol” in order to obtain what we desire (the date, sex), we insist in the opposite direction, seeking confirmation of what we do not want (the absence of the date), we will disconcert the sly one and corner her in the game. If she confirms the worst, she will have revealed the fact she had tried to hide: that she only wanted to use us to satisfy her selfishness. If she confirms the best, she will also have revealed what she had, likewise, tried to hide: that in truth she felt attraction or interest but did not want to admit it. In both cases, the sly one will have no way out and will be defeated in the little game. In these games (true wars!) of passion, the female tactic consists in hiding the truth about what is most important and central to our decision-making. Such strategic information is kept secret at all costs, and there is no use trying to wrest it from them by a direct and explicit route because, the more we insist along this route, the more they close up, block, and create a hell. The best thing, therefore, is to “strike them on the flanks,” that is, to attack the problem where they least expect it.
<!-- QUERY: "não queria dar o braço a torcer" (idiom: refuse to concede or admit being wrong) rendered "did not want to admit it"; alternative: "did not want to give in". -->

<!-- seg 04-0007 paragraph h=6b134608 -->
This tactical line really dismantles the little game because it operates on the psychological fields in which the woman is not prepared and in which she tries to avoid measuring inner strength with us. They are prepared for the insistence of the ignorant, who apply pressure in order to confirm male hopes and desires, but they are not prepared for the wise, who reaffirm and reinforce the female resistances themselves. The sly one does not know what to do when her stubborn blockages are accepted, reinforced, and reaffirmed by the man.
