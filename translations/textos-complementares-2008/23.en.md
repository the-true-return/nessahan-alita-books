22. Reinforcing the pillars of the theory

<!-- seg 23-0002 heading h=c4c21bcc -->
The research of Skyler S. Place

<!-- seg 23-0003 paragraph h=7e3ceaef -->
I recently learned of a study, carried out by Skyler Place (Indiana University), which showed that women, during “flirting,” behave in such a way as to make it difficult or impossible for men to be certain about any possible interest on their part. I was very pleased to learn that yet another experimental study proves what I have been saying for a long time.

<!-- seg 23-0004 paragraph h=0052dfd4 -->
Place demonstrated, by means of tests, how difficult it is to read female behavior while a man is approaching a woman, so as to conclude with certainty whether or not she likes the one approaching her. Women who were asked to observe the same behaviors of their peers and give an opinion on them also had difficulties. In short: Place demonstrated that it is difficult for men to know whether or not women are interested in them. The reverse was not found. The difficulty or impossibility for women of reading male behaviors is much smaller.

<!-- seg 23-0005 paragraph h=5ff127cb -->
If female behavior manifests itself in such a way as to prevent the man from drawing conclusions and proves difficult to interpret, this means that it is ambiguous. Place therefore demonstrated the truth of one of the central pillars of my theory: female behavioral ambiguity. From it derives the disconcerting paradoxicality that disorients man’s rational judgments by means of illogicality. It is difficult for us to conclude whether or not they want something with us because their behaviors are usually undefined. As I have said, indefiniteness is the fundamental weapon of the female sex in the war of passion. Place understands it as an evolutionary adaptive mechanism for selecting the best males of the species, in complete agreement with my theory in this respect. However, being a scientist, he neither broadens nor deepens the philosophical and moral implications of his finding. Nor does he give men ways of dealing with such difficulties, and he even goes so far as to justify women’s use of such a “weapon.”

<!-- seg 23-0006 paragraph h=2b77f830 -->
In truth, female behavioral ambiguity is not limited to the level of courtship by the man. It is also present in dating, in marriage, in stable and unstable relationships, and wherever the male’s sexual desire and the female’s acceptance are at stake. They usually behave in such a way as to confuse us, acting as if they liked and at the same time did not like us, as if they wanted us and at the same time did not want us. Simultaneously they attract and repel; they show themselves open and put up resistance. They send opposite and contradictory signals, which makes them hard to read. They never make things easy; they only stimulate and make things difficult, thus remaining in control of the situation. Examples? There are heaps of them: when she smiles marvelously and casts you meaningful glances, but says you “got it all wrong” when she is approached; when she tells you through tears that she loves you, but does exactly what you hate most; when she says she is faithful, but acts suspiciously; etc.

<!-- seg 23-0007 paragraph h=ceaee323 -->
In the face of ambiguous female behavior, I see two alternatives: 1) accept it and take advantage of everything possible; 2) force the definition in an unexpected direction, that is, accept and encourage the unpleasant pole of the undefined behavior, which is precisely what the sly one does not expect[^22]. The second option is only for those who are willing to risk losing the woman.

<!-- seg 23-0008 footnote n=22 h=7c4dd331 -->
Undefined behavior blends two opposite poles, one desirable and the other undesirable to the man. Manipulative women, as a rule, expect the man to insist on satisfying his desire, pressing for a definition in favor of the pleasant pole. However, if the man fights against himself and is able to press in the opposite direction, he will surprise them, striking them emotionally “on their unguarded flanks.”

The research of Jonason and Schmitt

<!-- seg 23-0010 paragraph h=f8538a89 -->
I have already mentioned their research in my book “Masculine Reflections on Woman and Love.” Peter Jonason and David Schmitt presented, in Japan, a study showing that women prefer men who carry the Dark Triad (liars, impulsive, and cheats). This study reinforced another pillar of my theory: the attraction to bad men. It is worth getting to know, and I recommend it.

The research of Dario Maestripieri

<!-- seg 23-0012 paragraph h=499af53c -->
This study, which also reinforced another pillar of my theories and was carried out by the Universities of Chicago and Santa Barbara, showed that women distinguish quickly and accurately, from facial features, men who like children from those who do not, and also distinguish those who have high levels of testosterone from those who do not. According to Maestripieri, one of the authors of the study, the most masculine men are preferred for short-term relationships, while those who like children are chosen for lasting commitments.

<!-- seg 23-0013 paragraph h=b2308af8 -->
There are several interesting implications and possible consequences of this study. If the most masculine specimens are good for short-term relationships, then they are good for sex without commitment, since the man will not accept a sexless relationship. And if the less masculine men who like children are preferred for lasting commitments, this means that they are good for being husbands and companions. In short: they want the latter for the shared life of marriage and the former for sex. The most masculine men get the best part: intense sex and freedom. The less masculine get the worst: worries about children, headaches, and commitments to an unreciprocated fidelity (and here I am reminded once again of Schopenhauer!). In my view, all this is also related to the little sexual attraction that husbands usually exert over their wives. The question then remains: whose children are being raised by the nice guys who like children?

Contraindications

<!-- seg 23-0015 paragraph h=8c008842 -->
My works should not be read by those who do not have a philosophical profile[^23] or who are not used to reasoning carefully and freely. They are completely contraindicated for anyone who lacks a deep capacity for interpretation and does not like to study. Nor do I recommend them to people whose understanding is sick[^24]. Such people will inevitably distort them and accommodate them to their defective worldviews, spoiling their messages. I cannot prevent them from approaching my works, but I can do my part by advising against their reading them.

<!-- seg 23-0016 footnote n=23 h=9a2d200f -->
I am referring to a love of philosophical reflection and not to an academic degree. For me, every human being is a philosopher, provided he appreciates serious reflection.

<!-- seg 23-0017 footnote n=24 h=04c1ef6d -->
I am referring to a sick understanding and not to an aching heart. Philosophical reflection can heal the soul, but it demands clarity of understanding. Sentimental illnesses can be cured starting from a healthy understanding.

<!-- seg 23-0018 paragraph h=6bd295a6 -->
Biased, dogmatic, extremist, violent, fanatical, intolerant people, adherents of one-sided and polarized views, who live for the enjoyment of the lower feelings and have turned stubborn partiality into a religion, are no good for learning or for reflecting on painful and polemical questions, because they do not have the nerves to bear reality without exploding. I have never written for that kind of people, and I want them far away from my texts.

<!-- seg 23-0019 paragraph h=f280a635 -->
The distorters do not possess the mental prerequisites necessary for understanding the dangerous, thorny, and polemical contents that interest us, for they are incapable of doubting the hegemonic paradigms of this decadent age, in which madness and dementia reign more than ever. To understand these contents, one needs to develop certain specific cognitive skills, among them the power to question one’s own convictions and the convictions of one’s age, to reason in reverse, to think dialectically, to change one’s ideas frequently, to go far beyond appearances and the obvious, and also to build knowledge continuously.

<!-- seg 23-0020 paragraph h=76c8f0fe -->
The sincere effort to understand something and not to irresponsibly close careless conclusions based on insufficient data does not depend on a person’s schooling but rather on his intellectual honesty. There are simple people who are very discerning when it comes to judging, and there are intellectual scoundrels, sly operators who manipulate information to defend lies. What matters is that we be sincere, rigorous, and careful while we weave our analyses.

<!-- seg 23-0021 paragraph h=a5f46733 -->
Therefore, if you are not willing to devote a large part of your time to perfecting your cognitive faculties, keep away from the study of problematic subjects and do not dare try to follow our reflections, because you will be getting in our way.

Although I have used this term in the past, I have now chosen to distinguish the representative from the collaborator, to avoid unpleasant and undue confusion. Sometimes I designate trusted friends, who move about the internet with ease, to publish my texts, books, and messages. They are only volunteer collaborators; they do not charge for their work, they do not answer for my opinions, and they cannot be considered my “official” public representatives, since they are only people who have offered to collaborate by faithfully passing on what I ask of them. Let “representative” be understood as a person authorized to express opinions on behalf of another. It is one thing to express one’s own opinions on behalf of another person, and quite another, certainly very different, to faithfully pass on the opinions of an author at the latter’s request. So far, I have not delegated the authorship of what I write, and my collaborators must in no way be confused with frauds who, every now and then, try to use another’s name for their own benefit. My collaborators are sincere helpers.
