6. The capricious judgments

<!-- seg 07-0002 paragraph h=3a2b307b -->
Female opinions, where romantic relationships are concerned, are often capricious, reckless, and lacking in logical-rational sense, which harms them and makes them childish.

<!-- seg 07-0003 paragraph h=d9bf35c0 -->
Try questioning them about the reasons for their incoherent conclusions and conduct. The answers will always be illogical, confused, subjective, and long. In truth, they do not really know why they choose complicated paths. The correct answer is this: because they draw their conclusions from feeling. For women, what is right is whatever provokes pleasant feelings, and what is wrong is whatever displeases them emotionally. They are emotionally oriented beings. This does not mean that they are innocent and loving, as everyone thinks. They are in truth very selfish, just as we are, but their selfishness is sentimental in content and, more specifically, romantic.

<!-- seg 07-0004 paragraph h=4565ad4e -->
Because they judge by emotion, they develop capricious and absurd opinions, such as, for example, that the worst males are the best and should be chased. None of them can properly explain why she does it. If we corner one of them in an argument, they will defend themselves by trying to provoke the most varied feelings in us: anger, pity, shame, doubt, confusion, desire, fear, etc. They will use a loud tone of voice to try to intimidate us, they will shout, they will cackle like witches to provoke feelings of smallness and ridicule, then they will cry so that we feel pity, then they will resort to cynical and provocative terms... These are wiles meant to manipulate our emotions, and they never change. Emotion is the terrain on which they rule and move with ease.

<!-- seg 07-0005 paragraph h=0100579e -->
Worthy of note is a very common tendency that can be seen in heated sexist arguments between people of the opposite sex, especially in cases where they are not in a romantic relationship with each other. This tendency becomes visible whenever we openly criticize female wiles, and it consists in attacking our masculinity by cynically calling us homosexuals. This is done in the moments of greatest desperation, when all other attempts at emotional manipulation have failed. Such attacks usually work: they disconcert and confuse the straight male, inducing him to worry about his own image and to try to prove that he is not what the sly one is pretending to think. While he chases after this nonsense, the argument is set aside. I have resolved these interesting cases simply by unmasking them and saying that women who challenge the masculinity of a heterosexual man are, in truth, challenging him to the sexual act, asking for sex. It usually works very well. Even so, the ideal is never to argue.

<!-- seg 07-0006 paragraph h=2c95e289 -->
When I state that female opinions are capricious, irrational, and irresponsible, many women become furious, but they should thank me, for I am denouncing something that harms them. If they made an effort to be a little more rational, without losing their superior emotionality and delicacy, they would be less reckless, less frivolous, less incoherent, they would not be so terrified of the truth, and they would live better, for they would be less prone to hysterical and depressive crises. Unfortunately, our little friends do not realize that the immense void of sadness and boredom in which they live is directly linked to the dirty games they play in love. Believing themselves very clever, they mistakenly suppose that deception is the road to happiness.

<!-- seg 07-0007 paragraph h=7cf54d3c -->
The experimentally verifiable sexual preference for those who do not love them and for promiscuous men is irrefutable proof that their conclusions are capricious and of purely subjective motivation. Add to this that such preferences are rewards for mediocrity and contribute to social degeneration.

<!-- seg 07-0008 paragraph h=013de66a -->
Stubborn and capricious opinions change only when the impact of their own mistakes strikes them in their feelings, causing suffering. Unfortunately, impacts are consequences and, therefore, are felt only <i>a posteriori</i>, when it is usually too late. That is why it is no use to alert, warn, caution, quarrel, etc., and still less to argue or debate.
