Philosophical irrationalism

<!-- seg 18-0087 paragraph h=943472da -->
Another philosophical current that influenced me was irrationalism. Irrationalism is opposed to rationalism and can be understood by inversion starting from it:

<!-- seg 18-0088 paragraph h=c7275466 -->
“[Rationalism] is opposed (...) to <i>irrationalism</i>, in all its forms (mysticism, occultism, philosophy of feeling, traditionalism).” (LALANDE, 1967, p. 842, my translation)

<!-- seg 18-0089 paragraph h=0161657b -->
Mysticism, occultism, and the philosophy of feeling are forms of irrationalism. Philosophical irrationalism, therefore, comes close to Gnosticism and spiritualism, standing opposed to the rationalist view, which in turn is very close to agnosticism and atheism. Schopenhauer is one of its exponents (NEW BARSA ENCYCLOPEDIA, 1998).

<!-- seg 18-0090 paragraph h=454fddb5 -->
According to the irrationalist view, reason is not the proper means for solving the crucial problems of existence, including the question of human suffering and pain. The fundamental existential questions (the meaning of life, death, love, happiness, etc.) transcend reason, which cannot encompass them and is powerless to solve them. Rationality is not a synonym for intelligence, just as irrationality is not a synonym for stupidity or foolishness. I use the word irrational in the following sense:

<!-- seg 18-0091 paragraph h=2ae6af3e -->
“Foreign or even contrary to <i>reason</i> (...). (LALANDE, 1967, p. 557, my translation)

<!-- seg 18-0092 paragraph h=7614d967 -->
“More especially, in É. Meyerson, that which, in the object of our knowledge, <b>exceeds our intellect</b>, the whole effort of the intellect being directed toward discovering the identical, and the content of our thought always presupposing a given diversity, without which there is no reality. The ‘irrational’ is thus a permanent limit to explanation and to intelligibility.” (LALANDE, 1967, p. 557, translation, emphasis, and bold mine)

<!-- seg 18-0093 paragraph h=d2d5d2d6 -->
The irrational is that which goes beyond reason and cannot be encompassed by it. According to irrationalism, irrationality is far removed from ignorance, stupidity, and foolishness, insofar as it does not correspond to something erroneous that reason can judge but rather to what is incomprehensible and disconcerting because it belongs to an order of things impenetrable to ordinary understanding. The irrational (or, if we prefer, the non-rational) hides an infinity of mysteries and systems that can only be accessed by non-rational forms of intelligence, not yet understood by Western man. People should not, therefore, consider this word offensive.

<!-- seg 18-0094 paragraph h=09b4382e -->
Non-rationality (or irrationality) can be seen in the intelligence of nature, in the behavior and generation of animals and plants, in the flowering of life. The human being, who is part of nature, is also an animal and possesses non-intellectual forms of intelligence in his unconscious psyche. In this sense, we can say that women’s non-rationality is an attribute of defense and dominance that nature has given them, conferring on them an immense advantage in relationships and allowing them to dominate man by way of the feelings.

<!-- seg 18-0095 paragraph h=99547ae5 -->
Rational intelligence is disconcerted by paradoxes and is not capable of understanding them. The simultaneity of mutually exclusive opposites is rejected by the intellect because it is not comprehensible; it exposes the limits of rationality and can only be understood through non-rational forms of intelligence. Obviously, none of this will be understood by those who believe irrationality to be a synonym for stupidity, for such a belief implies a gratuitous and a priori rejection of unknown logics. Whoever adopts it considers that there is no intelligence at all at work behind the illogicalities of human beings, which is false.

<!-- seg 18-0096 paragraph h=059ceb35 -->
Irrationalism has a strong presence in my writings and allowed me to grasp intuitively some highly effective (and, for that very reason, normally incomprehensible) irrational psychological mechanisms of manipulation and to develop ways of dismantling them. These mechanisms are almost invisible to the intellect when they are not translated into a rational language that makes it possible to visualize them or, at least, to imagine them coherently. This was the greatest contribution of irrationalism to my ideas: it allowed me to understand and expose the other side of the contradiction involving the question of intelligence.

By considering that being differs from thought and cannot be expressed in logical terms, realism comes close to irrationalism, for it considers that the real cannot be reached by the intellect and that being, in essence, cannot be thought. My point of view is that, if the real implies irrationality, we have to seek non-rational (irrational) means of cognition to reach it. In other words, we have to transcend formal logic and ordinary reasoning.

<!-- seg 18-0106 paragraph h=3df0a668 -->
The material world that reaches our senses is not what it seems to us. The senses deceive. A stone is not as we perceive it, because it contains much more than our poor sensory apparatus is capable of capturing. The world is, as Schopenhauer said, will and representation: will because desires determine the quality of what we perceive, and representation because we perceive only inner images.

<!-- seg 18-0107 paragraph h=4d20685d -->
What we perceive are limited inner representations of an external “something” that goes beyond the senses and reason. Our judgments and conclusions are projections of our wills (desires): the beautiful, the ugly, the high, the low, the right, the wrong, etc., are defined according to our desires. What we perceive outwardly is a projection of inner states, which correspond to the world of ideas (ideas are inner phenomena).

<!-- seg 18-0108 paragraph h=402deb6f -->
From the point of view of realism, I consider, for example, that beautiful people are not beautiful in themselves and that beauty is in the eye of the beholder, so that one and the same person may be considered beautiful or ugly simultaneously by different observers. The qualities of objects, beings, animals, and people do not exist objectively outside of us; they are attributed meanings (ideas) that prevail over what is sensible. A certain way of dressing or carrying oneself will cause a person to be seen as beautiful or ugly. The proof of the subjectivity of beauty lies in the fact that its criteria vary across time, space, cultures, and individuals. Therefore, beauty does not exist outside of man; it is an inner projection.

<!-- seg 18-0109 paragraph h=9f19c9d2 -->
Starting from realism, I consider that the material world is illusory, that the spiritual is the real, despite its unintelligibility, and that reality transcends the intellect. Platonic realism drew my attention to the possible existence of other logics.

<!-- seg 18-0110 paragraph h=a28530af -->
In Plato’s books, Socrates states several times that the passions confuse man and chain him to the world of illusions, that the soul freed from the chains of the physical body can see reality more fully, and that man must seek liberation from the instincts if he wishes to be happy after the death of the physical body. For Socrates, it is the clear understanding of reality that sets man free. And true understanding comes from absolutely sincere reflection. This is exactly my view, and I do not disagree with this venerable master, unjustly murdered by human ignorance.

Other influences

<!-- seg 18-0112 paragraph h=1c54602d -->
So as not to stretch the explanation out too much, I will mention other influences in a more summarized form.

Spiritualism, in its Gnostic-Theosophical-Rosicrucian-occultist-esoteric form, contributed by pointing to an alternative to blind, gratuitous belief in the invisible, lacking any proof, providing means for obtaining authentic knowledge of the “Great Beyond.”

<!-- seg 18-0115 paragraph h=6d2cf6b6 -->
Since I do not believe in absolute knowledge (and still less where the sphere of rational knowledge is concerned), we can say that there are many elements of philosophical relativism in the ideas I defend. Starting from Nietzschean perspectivism, I realized that the problems that interested me, the targets of my reflections, could be approached from different angles or points of view, and that the two poles or sides of a single problem that interested me could be subjected simultaneously to favorable and unfavorable criticism, there being, from a strictly logical (though not always formal) point of view, the possibility of the coexistence of several critical positions, often even antagonistic ones. In other words, my ideas reflect just one more perspective to be considered, alongside which other different but equally valid views can coexist. Those uninitiated in perspectivism suppose, for example, that unfavorable criticism of an object implies a favorable position toward its opposite, which is totally mistaken. They also suppose that it is impossible to make criticisms and adopt positions that are simultaneously favorable and unfavorable toward one and the same object, which is a naive conclusion. If I make unfavorable criticisms of the Greeks, that does not mean that I cannot make favorable criticisms of those very same Greeks, or equally unfavorable criticisms of the Trojans. In fact, anyone can make simultaneously favorable and unfavorable criticisms of the two poles of a problem, as long as they want to. This is a valid justification for the unrestricted freedom of criticism defended by Spinoza, for whom the suppression of the freedom to philosophize would destroy social peace. According to his thought, happiness would only be possible in the full expression of freedom of thought. Democratic political organization should be based on freedom of thought and opinion at all levels (Baruch Spinoza, Tractatus Theologico-Politicus, quoted in NEW BARSA ENCYCLOPEDIA, volume 13, 1998).

Restrictions on the “ultimatum”

<!-- seg 18-0146 paragraph h=ae8438fa -->
It is not advisable to use the “ultimatum”[^41] when we still have residues of amorous, passionate feelings for the woman because, by doing so, we risk falling into a ridiculous situation in which we are struck down by our own decision, thus giving rise to a tragicomedy, to the delight of the sly one, who will have a lot of fun with it. In this case, it will be we ourselves who have given her the chance to abandon us at the wrong time, that is, at a moment that is unfavorable to us. In general, this results in comical situations in which the man protests against the decisions he himself made. The ultimatum serves only for cases in which we can no longer stand the indefiniteness and want to put an end to the little games without any fear of losing our partner forever.
