8. The duplicity of feelings

<!-- seg 09-0002 paragraph h=a04db013 -->
Once in love, you will become dependent on the presence of your beloved and will pursue her incessantly so that your emotional pain may be relieved. Then the sly one will purposely and consciously set up a barrier to contact and keep you at a distance, suffering for love. The distance, the insurmountability of the barrier, the infatuation, and the pursuit stand in a directly proportional relationship to one another. The more in love you are, the more of a pursuer you will become. The more of a pursuer you are, the more insurmountable the barrier will be, the greater the distance, and the longer her absence will last.

<!-- seg 09-0003 paragraph h=2874b9d7 -->
It is a curious phenomenon: the sly one induces you to approach but then blocks the approach. By demanding the surrender of your heart, the sly one is inducing you to pursuit and courtship, for there is no passionate sentimentalism without a demand for the presence and nearness of the beloved object. Contradictory and illogical? No, just cold and calculating. What is the logic or meaning of such apparently contradictory behavior? To keep you emotionally enslaved, imprisoned by passion forever, loving her for all eternity, without being loved back and without receiving anything in return. Unconscious? No, purposeful and calculated. What is the motivation? Absolute sentimental selfishness. How is this possible? By the simple fact that we desire them desperately while they are almost indifferent to us.

<!-- seg 09-0004 paragraph h=48742d11 -->
How do we reverse it? By fighting against ourselves and acquiring characteristics that attract them. What are these characteristics? I have already described them exhaustively in my books.

<!-- seg 09-0005 paragraph h=a9186432 -->
Contradictory female conduct generates feelings of a contrary nature that do battle within us and tear our soul to pieces.

<!-- seg 09-0006 paragraph h=7cd2a963 -->
At the beginning of the relationship, when everything is a feigned bed of roses, feelings of attachment and the like are triggered and reinforced through the affectionate, caring, and loving conduct of one’s companion. In this phase, the woman behaves like a saint, ignores other males, etc. Later, when she has confirmed that we are well hooked and in love, our degree of dependence and emotional bond begins to be tested with provocative and challenging attitudes. It is in this stage that we experience many conflicts, most of them arising from conduct that subtly calls fidelity into question.

<!-- seg 09-0007 paragraph h=a7db1efc -->
Until we have taken the bait, the female pretends to be exactly what we desire. She acts like the lady of our loveliest dreams, perfect, wonderful, and divine. After we take the bait, however, her conduct changes little by little, and from paradise we fall into hell.

<!-- seg 09-0008 paragraph h=3045e819 -->
“Innocent” attitudes of attention, friendliness, closeness, and care toward other males irritate a man because they shake his conviction in the loyalty of his companion’s feelings. They are used on purpose as a form of provocation. The problem does not lie in the partner’s promiscuous conduct, as feminists try to make it appear, but in the lack of transparency, in the indefiniteness, in the confusion and doubt that the conduct arouses. If the conduct were clear and defined from the start, as in the case of the prostitute, there would be no problem. But since we are rational, the irritation of doubt, as Peirce says, eats away at us, causing great emotional suffering. We need defined situations. A relationship stuffed with question marks and poorly explained facts causes great torment, for we are left solely at the mercy of trust. Since irrational belief without a logical basis is not our strong point, we feel the ground give way beneath us. What enrages us are contradictory postures. Subtle conduct, apparently of no seriousness at all, is perceived by us as violent acts of disguised betrayal. The innocence is only apparent, for it is precisely subtle conduct without malice that begins betrayals. Knowing this, the sly ones do precisely what we detest, and they do it consciously and with premeditation.

<!-- seg 09-0009 paragraph h=0c313331 -->
Provocations do not happen only in the field of fidelity. There are also attitudes that challenge and affront our feelings and values in many other fields.

<!-- seg 09-0010 paragraph h=62a971b7 -->
Since we are territorial and want to protect our genes, we need to confirm continually through direct observation that our companion is absolutely faithful to us and keeps other males at a prudent distance. That is why, when the sly one complains, saying that we should trust her word, despite the evidence of facts that create doubts instead of certainties, we feel that we are being hoodwinked. The result is that we become enraged, with good reason, and little by little build up hostile and negative feelings toward the one we meant only to love. Such feelings do us great harm and, curiously, make them happy, because they are the proof that we are suffering over what they did.

<!-- seg 09-0011 paragraph h=bb09d0b7 -->
In the long run, then, a duplicity of feelings takes shape that confuses the nature of the relationship: we will nurture negative and, at the same time, positive feelings for one and the same person.

<!-- seg 09-0012 paragraph h=1170aee7 -->
This simultaneous duplicity destroys us because we can no longer define what we feel in order to polarize our attitudes. The positive feelings we naively created act as a brake that does not allow us to turn fully hostile toward them. The negative feelings prevent us from enjoying the fullness of the relationship. So we are split in two, cracked, loving and hating the same woman at the same time. The bomb explodes inside us, in the heart. The mistake, once again, consisted in letting ourselves get drunk on the poison of passion. If we had resisted the fascination, the beauty, the charm, the delicacy, we would not be pushed to the other extreme. Therefore, lust, attachment, admiration, longing, and other related feelings are defects as harmful as anger, fury, hatred, and jealousy. All of them must be exterminated from our soul through analysis, comprehension, assimilation, and prayer. I assert that, if you are an atheist, it will be harder to go beyond what you are.

<!-- seg 09-0013 paragraph h=69112636 -->
There is no way out other than freedom from passion. If you doubt it, try giving yourself over madly and you will see the disastrous results.

For the passionate man, I see only the following paths as possible:

<!-- seg 09-0021 paragraph h=3fdecbcd -->
The duplicity of feelings is closely bound up with the exploitative and opportunistic nature of human beings. When they feel they are losing us, they offer us love, but when they feel they are winning us, they offer indifference. As that master of politics, Machiavelli, rightly taught us, human beings are more inclined to take advantage of the love offered to them than to return it. Our companions are no exception to this law, and when they feel loved they regard the fact as an opportunity to be exploited to the full and not as an undeserved gift. The situation is all the more serious inasmuch as, again according to Machiavelli, we should give no grounds for hatred but only for fear. The solution is to keep reason on our side, to keep them from hating us, but to “punish” them when they abuse our tolerance and trust. Females should feel loved but not too much, protected but not completely, and should fear a pending little punishment whose intensity and limits are impossible to calculate. The “punishments,” in this case emotional ones, should be fair, short, and impactful. The benefits and rewards for good conduct should be handed out slowly over a good length of time, so that they are well savored and remembered for a long time.

<!-- seg 09-0022 paragraph h=fc210e5f -->
If we allow attempts at deception to go unanswered after they have been discovered, we will lose all credibility. Instead of recognizing the value of the noble reasons that moved us to forgive them (understanding, forgiveness, pity, compassion, etc.), females will take us for weak men, for their exploitative minds are not capable of understanding the value of noble feelings. They will repay our forgiveness with exploitative opportunism and not with gratitude, saying to themselves: <i>“What a weak man! He doesn’t have the nerve to stop me and passively lets me abuse his tolerance and trust.”</i> On the other hand, if we “punish” them accurately in the emotional field, making them suffer as they make us suffer, giving everything back to them while at the same time explicitly laying bare the crooked trick they pulled, they begin to admire us deep down, even if they cry, lament, and protest. It follows, therefore, that the crueler and more vengeful are more admired than the merciful and compassionate, unfortunately. Once again it is thus demonstrated that men in love ruin themselves.

<!-- seg 09-0023 paragraph h=925a101c -->
Note that provocative attitudes (lies, attempts to deceive, manipulate, double-cross, etc.) are disguised and take on an innocent appearance. Besides, they tend to appear precisely when the atmosphere between the couple is wonderful, for females have not the slightest respect for the well-being of relationships. In truth, they see the wonderful atmosphere of well-being as an opportunity to be seized, that is, a sign that the most propitious moment has come to double-cross us, for it is the moment when we are most pliable and “nice.” They believe they must seize this moment as soon as possible. This is the reason why they suddenly spoil good times together. They tend to surprise us by spoiling our good moments with negative attitudes when we are friendly. They cultivate our expectation of certain attitudes and surprise us with the opposite attitudes, making the duplicity of feelings inevitable. Our feelings, sincerity, and trust are seen as objects to be used without the slightest consideration. Hence the importance of armoring ourselves at this level.

<!-- seg 09-0024 paragraph h=02565189 -->
Observe, moreover, the behavior of your companion and you will discover many lies and disguised manipulations. You will also discover many sophisticated wiles for undermining distrust and inducing you to credulity. How can you feel only love for a woman like that? It is not possible, for she is provoking love and hatred at the same time. Therefore, there is no way out: the only way to avoid the duplicity of feelings is to have no feeling at all, neither good nor bad!

<!-- seg 09-0025 paragraph h=9dcc8af6 -->
Provocations are a thermometer. Curiously, by provoking your fury, your partner will be measuring the intensity of your passion. She will have the exact measure of your feelings, because submission occurs in inverse proportion to your capacity to be hostile to her, hurt her, and mistreat her. Your powerlessness to cause her any kind of harm reveals how much you like her and desire her. Passionate love acts as a brake on hatred, imposing a limit on destructive attitudes. That is why pure male hatred, unmixed with passion, terrifies them so much.

<!-- seg 09-0026 paragraph h=16b0320a -->
Through tests and observations, the limits of husbands and boyfriends become known. If all goes well, that is, according to selfish female plans, the provocations should never exceed the limits of aggressive and murderous male fury. When that happens, a tragedy takes place, and it was due to a miscalculation by the woman or to some unforeseen event that revealed dangerous secrets. Of course, the aggressive attitudes of men who lose their heads are wrong and we do not approve of them, but it so happens that, very often, it is the woman herself who provokes them.

<!-- seg 09-0027 paragraph h=91bb2373 -->
Irritating provocations are closely related to a behavioral tendency that makes them absolutely resistant to control: the habit of doing exactly what they perceive we do not want them to do. We dismantle this vice if we become “slippery” and disconcerting. We become disconcerting when we talk little and agree with everything, but at the same time sabotage everything and cooperate with nothing, leaving them to act alone while we observe them “from the outside.”

<!-- seg 09-0028 paragraph h=b8a519da -->
When she is unable to cause romantic suffering and definitively concludes that she will never win the war of passion, the woman will wait for a strategic moment that allows a sudden withdrawal leaving strong marks, memories, and expectations hanging in the air. In a last desperate act, she will show herself wonderful in every sense in the hope of leaving a very strong mark on the man’s memory and feelings, in an attempt to induce him to get drunk on it, before leaving him without warning. If you weaken and get drunk on this poison, she will then go away without freeing you from these chains, so that you suffer forever. It will have been your last time. Somehow, through the most varied devices, your goddess-demon will keep hopes alive in your mind to induce you to wait for all eternity. Perhaps she will lie, saying that one day she will come looking for you again. Perhaps she will say clearly that she does not want you anymore but will purposely smile sweetly or, hypocritically, appear worried about you and caring, so that your hope is kept alive, which constitutes an act of bad faith. Certainty, definition, and clarity will be denied intentionally and consciously. Deep down, despite appearances, she will find a way not to free you, to leave you waiting. And she will not feel like a criminal, for her nature will not allow her to understand the horror of the emotional crime she has committed. Note that they act as if we had no feelings and, without hesitation, offend what is most important and dear to us: the love we feel for them.

<!-- seg 09-0029 paragraph h=1b6b037a -->
The duplicity of feelings arises from contradictory behaviors that feed or stimulate male hopes and then frustrate them, playing these opposites off alternately. The examples are very well known, but I will cite them once more: responding to a man’s courtship, giving him hope, only to reject the suitor as soon as he approaches; accepting a phone number, implying that she will call, and not calling, so that we are left waiting; making a date and not showing up, giving incoherent excuses; appearing emotionally committed and at the same time letting signs of infidelity show through, etc. The attempt to confuse in order to immobilize and disarm is a constant.

As always, the solution for getting out of these hells is to rise above these bestial emotions by attaining a higher state of consciousness. Romantic love is as stupid as hatred, because both are passionate and subjective and do not allow us to see reality.
